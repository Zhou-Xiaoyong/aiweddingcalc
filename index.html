Frequently Asked Questions

The average wedding in the US costs between $30,000 and $35,000 as of 2026, with the national average at about $33,000 for 100-150 guests. However, costs vary dramatically by location — New York and California weddings average $45,000+, while Midwest weddings can be under $25,000. The biggest factors are guest count, location, and formality level.
A reasonable wedding budget depends on your financial situation and priorities, but here are general guidelines: Budget ($15K-$20K) — backyard/casual, fewer guests, lots of DIY. Moderate ($25K-$40K) — semi-formal, 100-150 guests, professional vendors. Upscale ($40K-$75K) — formal, 150-200 guests, premium venue and vendors. Luxury ($75K+) — black tie, designer everything, full production. The key is to set a budget you're comfortable with and stick to it.
Creating a wedding budget in 5 steps: 1) Determine total available funds (yours + family contributions). 2) Finalize your guest count — this drives most costs. 3) Research average costs in your area. 4) Allocate by category using our budget calculator. 5) Build in a 5-10% buffer for unexpected expenses. Track every dollar as you book vendors and don't be afraid to adjust — if one category goes over, take from another.
Most couples begin planning 12-18 months before the wedding. This gives you time to book popular venues and vendors, who often book up 6-12 months in advance. Here's a rough timeline: 12+ months out — book venue and set budget. 9-12 months — book major vendors (photographer, caterer, DJ). 6-9 months — book attire, flowers, invitations. 3-6 months — final details, fittings, menu tasting.
A typical wedding budget breakdown for 150 guests: Venue & Catering 45-50% (the largest expense), Photography & Videography 10-12%, Attire & Beauty 8-10%, Music & Entertainment 6-8%, Flowers & Decor 6-8%, Stationery 2-3%, Transportation & Rentals 3-5%, Favors & Gifts 2-3%, Officiant & Marriage License 1-2%, and Buffer/Emergency Fund 5-10%. Always build in a buffer for unexpected costs.
Top ways to save money on your wedding: 1) Choose a non-Saturday date (Friday/Sunday saves 10-25%). 2) Limit the guest count. 3) Opt for buffet or family-style catering instead of plated. 4) Do DIY decor and favors. 5) Skip the videography or hire a newer photographer building their portfolio. 6) Use grocery store flowers. 7) Book an all-inclusive venue to avoid hidden fees. 8) Limit plus-ones to married/engaged couples only.
Catering costs per guest vary widely by style and location. On average in 2026: Buffet $50-75/person, Family Style $60-85/person, Plated Dinner $65-145/person, Food Stations $55-120/person, Cocktail Reception $35-85/person. These prices usually don't include service charge (18-22%) and tax (7-10%), so add 25-30% to get your final catering cost.
Wedding costs vary dramatically across the US. New York and New Jersey are the most expensive at $47,000–$50,000 on average — about 50% above the national average. California and Massachusetts follow at $43,000–$45,000. On the lower end, Midwest states like Ohio, Michigan, and Wisconsin average $27,000–$28,000 — roughly 15-18% below the national average of $33,000. Location is the single biggest factor in your wedding budget.
Most expensive (2026 averages): New York ($50K), New Jersey ($47K), Rhode Island ($46K), Massachusetts ($45K), Connecticut ($44.5K), California ($43K). Most affordable: Wisconsin ($27K), Michigan ($27.5K), Ohio ($28K), Tennessee ($27.5K), South Carolina ($28K), North Carolina ($28.5K). The difference between the most and least expensive states is nearly 85% — location matters more than any other factor.
