A Thanksgiving weekend wedding seats about 123 guests out of 168 invited, and the same list in September seats 127. Four seats is the smallest number in this article. The bigger one is what those seats cost, because the plate is $125.75 once service and tax attach to it, and the room discount that made the date attractive tends to leave through a different door.

Couples arrive at this weekend from two directions. Some got engaged over Thanksgiving dinner last year and picked the anniversary, which puts them in the middle of Engagement Season with a very short planning runway. Others chose it because late November looks like an empty calendar, and a hall that costs $6,200 in September is quoted at $4,340 in November. Both groups are working with the same attendance math, and almost nobody runs it before the invitations go out.

Why a Thanksgiving Weekend Wedding Loses Guests

The loss is not spread evenly. It comes almost entirely from one group: the people who have to get on a plane.

Local guests behave the same in November as they do in June. So do the ones within a two-hour drive. Flying guests do not, for reasons that have nothing to do with how much they like you. The Wednesday through Sunday around Thanksgiving is the most expensive domestic airfare window of the year, and a guest who already spent that money to see family in November is being asked to do it twice in thirteen months. Plenty of them say yes in July, mean it, and book nothing.

There is a second effect that shows up in the RSVP data rather than the bank account. Thanksgiving is when the two families first sit down together after a fall proposal, which means the list gets built at the table, out loud, with aunts adding cousins. The list grows in a room where nobody is looking at a per-head price. A 168-name draft that came out of one dinner is normal, and so is the surprise when the caterer's proposal arrives at $95 a plate.

What the holiday does not change: local acceptance, vendor availability in most markets, or the venue's willingness to deal. Late November is genuinely soft for banquet halls, which is why the site fee moves. It is not soft for resorts, destination properties, or anything within driving distance of a beach, where the four-day weekend is the product being sold and the rate reflects it.

How Many Guests Will Actually Show Up

Split the list by travel distance before you do anything else. Three groups, three different acceptance rates, and the holiday only bends one of them.

The 50% figure is the one that looks wrong until it happens. It is the same rate planners use for a destination wedding, and a holiday weekend behaves like one for anybody who has to buy a ticket. Apply it only to the flying group and the estimate stays honest; apply it to the whole list and you will under-cater by twenty plates.

This is also why the RSVP list is not the number to cook for. On the same 168 names, 141 people reply and 132 say yes. A 132 count would put you 9 plates over, or $1,132 at the all-in rate. The reply rate itself is normal: about 84% of guests answer, and the 27 who stay silent are not all coming. What the Date Costs Against a September Saturday

Here is the part that decides whether the holiday weekend was a good idea. Same 123 guests, same menu, same room, two different Saturdays.

The discount is real. It is also the only line in the table that moves your way. Thirty percent off the room is $1,860 in your pocket, and then a November wedding spends $1,480 heating a tent, $246 hanging coats, and $300 more on flowers because nothing is in season locally. Net: $166 more for the holiday weekend, or $1.35 a guest.

Two assumptions sit inside that number, and both are worth checking against your own plan. The heaters disappear entirely if the ceremony and dinner both happen indoors, which turns the $166 back into a $1,314 saving. The coat check is a real line at any November event with an outdoor walk, usually $1 to $3 a guest, and venues that do not staff it will still send someone to hang forty coats for free, badly.

Zoom out and the block above is $26,034 of a $34,200 wedding, or 76% of the total. Everything else in a typical budget (attire, photography, DJ, stationery, cake, rings, officiant, favors) is fixed by contract and does not care what month you chose. That is why a date decision feels enormous at the venue tour and measures as small change at the end: the movable portion of a wedding budget is narrower than the discount language suggests. Run your own version through the catering calculator and the venue cost calculator before you compare two quotes, since only one of them will include the heat. If the menu is still open, our Thanksgiving wedding menu cost breakdown explains why the turkey dinner prices $16 a head above the chicken plate it replaced.

One more line that never appears on a proposal and always appears on a November invoice: light. The Saturday after Thanksgiving is the shortest daylight Saturday most couples will ever book. Sunset runs from about 4:20 p.m. in Seattle to 5:30 p.m. in Miami, and earlier than that in the Northeast and Midwest. A 3:00 p.m. ceremony leaves roughly 90 minutes of usable outdoor light, so any portrait plan that assumes golden hour has to be rebuilt around a 2:30 start or a flash setup.

The 72-Hour Guarantee on a Thanksgiving Weekend Wedding

Most caterers want a guaranteed headcount 72 hours before the event, and the guarantee is what you pay for whether or not the chairs fill. This is where a nervous couple turns a $12,000 contract into a $12,350 one.

Start with the true plate price. A $95 dinner is not $95 once the contract finishes with it. Add the 22% service charge and then 8.5% sales tax on the food and the service charge, and each seated guest bills $125.75. That is the number to multiply by every empty chair.

Guarantee Signed Food Billed Against a $12,000 Food Minimum Cost of the Buffer
123, the expected count$11,685Billed at the $12,000 floor$0
127, a four-seat buffer$12,065$65 above the floor$65
130, a seven-seat buffer$12,350$350 above the floor$350

Seven empty covers at $125.75 is $880, so why does the buffer only cost $350? Because 123 guests at $95 already fall $315 short of the $12,000 food minimum. That shortfall is money you owe no matter what, and adding guests fills a hole you were going to pay for anyway. Your buffer is effectively free until you clear the minimum.

The same seven seats cost the full $880 at a venue with a $9,000 minimum, or with 140 on the list. So the rule is not "always pad" or "never pad". Size the buffer against the gap: if 123 guests leaves you $315 under the minimum, padding five to seven covers costs almost nothing and buys real protection. If you are already $2,000 over the minimum, every padded seat is a full-priced empty chair.

Under-guaranteeing has a price too, and it is smaller than most couples assume. Walk-ins get fed at the contracted rate plus an administrative fee that runs $25 to $50 per head, so two surprise guests on that $95 plate land at about $240 to $290. Two or three seats of buffer, not seven, is the right hedge.

How to Protect the Count

Four moves, all of them cheaper than the mistakes they prevent.

  • Send save-the-dates eight to ten months out. The standard window is six to eight, and it does not apply to a holiday weekend. Thanksgiving flights and rental houses get booked close to a year ahead, and a guest choosing between your wedding and a family trip needs the date in hand before they commit. Our guide to when to send save-the-dates has the full timeline.
  • Take a courtesy block or no block at all. Hotels do not discount a peak holiday weekend, so the group rate can land at or above the public rate on the same rooms. A standard block with an 80% pickup requirement then bills you for whatever nobody books. Ask for a courtesy block, which holds rooms at the public rate with no attrition penalty, and put the request in the contract email.
  • Set the RSVP deadline four weeks out, not two. About 84% of guests reply without being chased, which leaves 27 people to find on a 168-name list. Chasing starts three weeks out, and the early deadline gives you a week of slack before the guarantee is due.
  • Hold the B-list until you have declines in hand. Invitations go out 8 to 12 weeks before the wedding, which puts a holiday-weekend mailing in early September. Anyone you add after that gets a rushed envelope and knows exactly why.

The reply card is the cheapest place to fix a count problem, because the wording does the work. A line that reads "We have reserved 2 seats in your honor" ends the plus-one negotiation before it reaches your inbox, and a hard reply date gives you something to point at when the text messages start. Our invitation wording examples cover the reply-card language that holds up, and the kids meal guide handles the under-12 seats that quietly reprice a holiday dinner.

The count also decides Thursday. Guests who flew in have nowhere to be the night before Thanksgiving, and the dinner that absorbs them runs bigger than any rehearsal dinner you planned for. If 60 of your 123 travelers are staying through the weekend, expect a welcome dinner list near 72 and a check near $5,992. If the date is still open, price both versions before you commit. A four-day weekend is a real gift to local guests and a real cost to everyone else, and the two effects do not balance by accident. Build the list at 168, cook for 123, guarantee 127, and let the venue's $1,860 discount pay for the heaters first. If your own numbers land within $200 of a September Saturday, the holiday weekend was a scheduling decision and not a budget one, and that is the version worth signing. One more variable sits on top of this ladder: which of the four dates you pick. A Friday date costs locals a day off and seats about 78 of a 120-name list, Sunday seats 85, and Thanksgiving Day itself seats 53 because a share of the list is hosting its own dinner.
