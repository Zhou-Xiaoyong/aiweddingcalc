If you're planning a wedding and watching every dollar, one decision quietly shapes your entire budget before you book a single vendor: the month you pick. Couples ask us all the time what the cheapest month to get married really is, and the answer isn't vague luck — it's supply and demand, written into every venue's pricing calendar. Get the timing right and you can trim thousands off a celebration that looks just as beautiful as a peak-season Saturday.

The national average US wedding now runs about $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study). A meaningful chunk of that is the venue and the vendors tied to it — and those prices swing by 20–40% depending on the date you choose. This guide breaks down the 2026 pricing landscape month by month, shows you exactly where the savings hide, and helps you decide whether an off-peak date is right for you.

The Cheapest Months to Get Married, Ranked

Across most US markets, venue and vendor pricing clusters into three tiers. Here's the general pattern couples see when they start requesting quotes:

  • Deep discount (cheapest): January, February, March. Venues often cut site fees 20–30% to fill the slowest stretch of the year, and many photographers, florists, and DJs have open calendars they're happy to discount.
  • Moderate discount (shoulder): April, early May, late August, November (after the Thanksgiving holiday week). Expect 5–15% softer pricing than peak, especially midweek.
  • Premium (most expensive): June, September, October, and the holiday-adjacent weeks of December. These are the dates everyone wants, and prices reflect it.

So if you want the single cheapest month to get married, January and February are the safest bets, with March a close, slightly milder third. The trade-off is weather and daylight — which is exactly why many budget-conscious couples land on a mild early-spring or late-fall date as their sweet spot.

Why Some Months Cost Less (and Why That's Good for You)

The discount isn't a gimmick — it's basic economics. Venues have fixed overhead whether a room is booked or empty, so when demand drops in winter they lower rates to fill dates. Vendors who are booked solid from May through October suddenly have gaps in February they'd rather discount than lose.

A few mechanics drive the gap:

  • Venue site fees — the biggest lever, typically 35–45% of your total budget. This is where off-peak savings are largest.
  • Catering minimums — many venues waive or lower food-and-beverage minimums in slow months, which drops your per-plate math.
  • Vendor availability — popular photographers and bands charge less when they aren't turning away higher-paying peak dates.
  • Floral costs — in-season blooms in spring and summer are cheaper than forced winter imports, though this cuts both ways depending on your palette.

The Months to Avoid If You're on a Budget

If saving money is the goal, the months to approach with caution are the ones everyone else wants:

  • June — the classic "wedding month," still the most-booked in many regions.
  • September & October — the modern peak, beloved for mild weather and fall color, which pushes prices to their annual high.
  • December (holiday weeks) — beautiful, but venues often charge premiums for the 20th–31st, and you compete with corporate holiday parties for the same spaces.
  • Valentine's week (mid-February) — a small but real spike inside an otherwise cheap month; if you want February savings, aim for earlier or later in the month.

None of this means you can't get married in October — plenty of couples do, and love the result. It just means a peak month is the one place where "cheapest month to get married" thinking goes out the window, and you'll pay for the privilege.

How Much You Actually Save by Picking the Cheapest Month to Get Married

Numbers help more than vibes here. For a wedding budgeted near the $34,200 national average, shifting from a peak October Saturday to a January or February date commonly saves $3,000–$8,000 once venue, catering, and photography discounts stack. A smaller 50-guest celebration sees a lower absolute saving but a similar percentage — often 15–25% off the venue-and-F&B core.

The breakdown usually looks like this:

  • Venue: $1,500–$4,000 saved from discounted site fees and lower F&B minimums.
  • Photography & video: $500–$1,500 saved as vendors discount open winter dates.
  • Florals & decor: $300–$1,000 saved when using in-season or pared-down winter palettes.
  • Entertainment: $200–$800 saved on DJs and bands with gaps in their calendar.

Day of Week and Time of Day Matter Too

The month is the biggest lever, but it's not the only one. Two more choices stack on top of it:

  • Day of week: A Friday or Sunday wedding in an off-peak month can run 10–20% cheaper than a Saturday in that same month; a weekday wedding can go even lower. The deepest discount is almost always an off-peak month plus a non-Saturday day.
  • Time of day: Brunch and lunch receptions use venues during lower-demand windows and skip the dinner F&B premium. They're a favorite money-saving move for couples who picked a pricey region but a cheap month.

These levers work because they attack the same thing — venue demand — from different angles. Pick all three (cheap month, non-Saturday, off-peak meal) and you've built the most discount-friendly date possible without sacrificing the guest experience.

How to Lock In Off-Season Savings

Finding a cheap month is step one; keeping the discount is step two. A few practical moves:

  1. Get the quote in writing with the date attached. Seasonal rates live in venues' pricing sheets — ask specifically for the off-peak number so it can't "expire" during contract review.
  2. Book 12–18 months out. Even off-peak dates for popular venues go, and early booking preserves today's rate.
  3. Ask vendors about winter packages. Many photographers and planners quietly offer "off-season" bundles that aren't advertised on their main pricing page.
  4. Watch the holiday trap. Confirm your date isn't inside a premium holiday week before you celebrate the discount.
  5. Model it before you commit. Compare a peak vs. off-peak scenario in the Wedding Cost Estimator so you know the real dollar difference for your guest count and state.

Regional pricing matters too: the off-peak discount is deeper in cold-climate markets and shallower in warm destinations where "winter" is still prime season. Frequently Asked Questions

Is the cheapest month the same in every state?

Not exactly. The off-peak pattern holds nationally, but the size of the discount varies. Cold-weather states see the steepest winter drops, while warm destinations (think parts of Florida, Arizona, and Southern California) have a milder off-season and smaller savings. Always request the venue's actual seasonal sheet for your region.

Will an off-peak wedding feel "less special"?

Only if you let it. Winter and early-spring weddings have a cozy, intimate character that many guests love, and you'll have more vendor attention and flexibility. The "special" comes from the people and the details, not the calendar page.

What if my dream venue is still pricey in the off-season?

Then combine the cheap month with a Friday/Sunday or brunch format, or consider a non-traditional venue (park pavilions, restaurants, museums) where seasonal premiums are smaller. The Venue Cost Calculator helps you compare options on equal footing.
