A 120-guest Thanksgiving weekend wedding on Sunday, November 29, 2026 bills $30,471. Same room, same vendors, same 120 names on Thanksgiving Day itself: $37,054. Divided by the people who actually show up, that is $358 a guest against $699.

Thanksgiving falls on November 26 this year, which puts four bookable dates in one four-day stretch. Couples usually pull the site fee for each, see that Sunday is cheapest, and stop there. The site fee is the smallest of the four lines that move, and on the Friday date it is the only line that moves down at all.

Below is the whole ledger, priced off the $34,200 US average for the Saturday, November 28 date. Every number reconciles, and every number is one you can ask a venue to confirm on a proposal.

The Labor Rate, Not the Room, Sets the Date

Caterers write the holiday rate as a labor multiplier: 1.5 on Thanksgiving Day, 1.25 on the Friday and Saturday after, and standard rate on Sunday. Labor is about 39% of a plate, so the multiplier touches a little less than half your food bill and none of the room.

A $95 plate carries $37.05 of standard labor. Moving from 1.0x to 1.25x adds $9.26 a head, and that $9.26 does not stay $9.26. The 22% service charge and the 8.5% sales tax are both calculated on the subtotal that already contains it, so the real bump is $12.27 a head, or $1,080 across 88 plates.

Two multipliers stack the same way. Saturday against Sunday is $12.27 a guest. Thursday against Sunday is $24.52. Neither number appears anywhere on a menu page, and both are larger than the entire difference between the Friday and Saturday room fees.

Ask the question in this exact form: does the multiplier apply to labor only, and is the service charge calculated on the subtotal before or after it? A caterer who applies 1.25x to the whole invoice instead of the labor line charges you for the privilege of having chairs.

The Food Minimum Binds Before the Site Fee Does

Every date carries a food and beverage minimum, and the four numbers do not move in the same direction as the room. On this contract they read $13,000 for Thursday, $10,000 for Friday, $12,000 for Saturday, and $9,000 for Sunday, measured on the all-in food total.

Saturday's 88 plates at $138.02 come to $12,146. That clears the $12,000 minimum by $146. Drop two guests and the minimum is what you pay, which makes the 87th and 88th plates free. A couple who cuts the list to save money that weekend discovers the last two seats were already bought.

Thursday is the reverse, and it is where that date is actually decided. Sixty guaranteed plates at $150.27 is $9,016. The holiday minimum is $13,000, so you pay $13,000, which is 87 plates. Fifty-three people eat. The room fee on Thursday looks like a $2,000 premium. The minimum is the line that turns a 53-guest dinner into a 120-guest invoice.

This is also why the Sunday minimum rarely saves what it promises. At 85 plates you spend $10,689 against a $9,000 floor, so the floor never binds. The Sunday saving comes from the room fee and the labor rate, not from the minimum. If a venue quotes you a low Sunday minimum as the reason to book, ask what the all-in food total is at your guest count.

Which Thanksgiving Weekend Wedding Date Your List Can Fill

Guest count is the multiplier on every per-head line, and it moves 35 people across these four dates. The starting ladder is the one this site uses everywhere: local guests accept at 85%, guests driving 50 to 300 miles at 65%, and guests who fly at 50% during the most expensive airfare window of the year.

On 120 names split 66 local, 34 driving, and 20 flying, Saturday seats 88. That is 73% of the list, and it is the number to build the room around.

  • Friday seats about 78. A Friday wedding costs your local guests a PTO day in a week most of them already took off, so the local rate drops to roughly 75% and flyers to 45%.
  • Sunday seats about 85. Locals and drivers hold steady. Flyers drop to about 35%, because the only flight home that gets them to work Monday leaves Sunday evening, mid-reception.
  • Thanksgiving Day seats about 53. A meaningful share of the list is hosting its own dinner that afternoon. The guests who do come are the ones who matter most, and there are 53 of them in a room built for 120.

The Thanksgiving weekend guest count breakdown runs the same ladder against a 168-name list and the guarantee clause, and the RSVP calculator turns replies into a plated number you can hand a caterer. Neither one adjusts for the day of the week, which is the piece you have to do yourself.

Where the Friday Discount Goes

Friday's room is $900 under Saturday and the invoice is $2,280 under. That reads like free money until you divide by 78 instead of 88. Friday costs $409 a guest against Saturday's $389.

Part of the gap is the photographer. A 12% holiday fee on a $3,100 package is $372, and most photographers apply it to Thursday, Friday, and Saturday, then drop it for Sunday. On Friday that fee eats 41% of the room discount before you have paid for a single plate.

Friday still has one real argument, and it is not price. Your out-of-town list is already in town for Thanksgiving, so a Friday wedding converts a family visit into a wedding instead of asking everyone to book a second trip in December. If that is the reason you want Friday, budget it as a 78-guest wedding from the first draft. Ordering 88 plates to be safe on a date that seats 78 is $1,380 of food nobody eats.

The other Friday trap is the bakery. The Saturday after Thanksgiving is the busiest delivery day of the month for most bakeries, and a Friday cake has to be baked Wednesday, which some shops price as a rush order. Ask when the cake is baked, not just when it is delivered.

How to Choose the Date

Four questions, in this order. They take one email each and they decide the date more reliably than a site visit.

  • Get the food minimum per date in writing. Not the room fee. The minimum is the line that changes whether a Thursday wedding is a 53-guest dinner or a 120-guest invoice.
  • Ask what the labor multiplier attaches to, and where the service charge sits. Labor only, applied before the 22%, is the version you want. Whole-invoice, applied after, adds roughly $1,500 on this contract.
  • Ask the photographer for the holiday policy in writing. 12% on three of the four dates is normal. A shop that charges it on Sunday too is telling you something about how it prices everything else.
  • Set the guarantee off the date's attendance, not the invite list. Saturday at 88, Sunday at 85, Friday at 78. Then check the number against the minimum before you sign, because the two clauses interact.

Then decide what a seat is worth to you. Sunday is $358 a guest and $3,729 cheaper in total. Saturday is $389 a guest with three more people in the room and a send-off that does not end on a work night. If the 85 who come on Sunday are the 85 you would have wanted in a 120-person room anyway, take the $3,729 and put it toward the honeymoon. If the list includes 30 people who can only make a Saturday, you are paying $31 a head for them, and that is the cheapest part of the whole wedding.
