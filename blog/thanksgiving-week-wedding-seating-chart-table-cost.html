Two cousins called this morning to say they are driving up from Dallas after all. That makes 90 for Saturday, and your eleven 60-inch rounds seat exactly 88. A twelfth table costs $242 in fixed charges before one plate of food goes on it, which puts those two guests at $226 each against the $105 a head you signed in August.

There is a cheaper way to seat them. It costs $18 and it is covered a few sections down.

The seating chart is the last document of Thanksgiving week that still changes, and the only one where changing your mind has a price attached. Everything else on the list is a phone call.

What One More Table Costs, Line by Line

Rentals are billed per item, catering per head, flowers per table. One extra table pulls from all three invoices at once, which is why the number surprises people who have budgeted per guest since March.

A full table costs less per head than your $389 average, because the venue and the band are already paid for. It only gets expensive when it stays mostly empty.

Why the Ninth Guest at a New Table Costs $226

This is the part that trips up spreadsheets. A wedding's seating cost is not a line. It is a staircase, and you pay for the whole step whether or not anyone stands on it.

Guests attending Tables Fixed Food Added Per added guest
88 (planned) 11 $0 $0 $0 Baseline
90 (two cousins) 12 $242 $210 $452 $226
96 (eight more yeses) 12 $242 $840 $1,082 $135

Those two guests pay for 4.3 plates of food and for the entire table around them. Fill that table and the marginal guest falls to $135. Fill the table you already have before ordering another one.

The caterer's side of the number is the one you cannot argue with. The guarantee, usually due 72 hours out, is the count you pay for whether or not those people walk in. Buy Seats, Not Tables: The $18 Swap

Two 60-inch rounds become two 72-inch rounds. The table goes from $16 to $22, the linen from $24 to $27, and each one seats two more people. That is $9 a table for two extra seats, or $18 total for four.

Nine tables of eight plus two tables of ten seats 92. You need 90. Every cousin has a chair, the chart stops being a hostage negotiation, and you never call the rental company again.

Three things have to be true. The venue's posted occupancy governs, not the number in your contract. The caterer has to serve ten at a 72-inch round, which they will, because that is the standard. And the centerpiece has to fit a 12-inch footprint, so a low bowl or a cluster of tapers, not the tall arrangement that looked good in June.

If the room cannot take 72s, the next cheapest seat is at the table you already have. A 60-inch round will hold nine if you drop chargers and move the centerpiece to a cocktail-height vase. That is free, at the price of 2.6 inches of elbow room a person. Ask the caterer to set it, not the venue, because the caterer is the one plating.

Do not order the twelfth table for two people and hope the count grows. If it does not, you have two guests at a table with a $121 centerpiece nobody sits near, in the corner that photographs badly. The venue capacity guide covers how many rounds actually fit before the aisle disappears.

Why Thanksgiving Week Breaks the Normal Seating Math

Every planning checklist says to finalize seating two to three weeks out. That advice assumes the people you are calling are at their desks. This week they are not.

Three things happen at once. The rental warehouse loads trucks Wednesday and closes Thursday and Friday, so the table count is fixed 72 hours out. The caterer's office runs on two people Friday and answers nothing after noon Wednesday. And your guest list starts moving again, because the Sunday after Thanksgiving is the most expensive travel day of the year.

The movement runs both ways. Out-of-town guests no-show at 15% to 20% on a holiday weekend. Flights get repriced, a parent gets sick, a plus-one cannot get Monday off. Local guests no-show at 5% to 8%, mostly for reasons nobody explains. On 88 attending, expect six to eight empty chairs. That is normal.

What is not normal is the walk-in. Holiday weekends produce them, because family is already in town and the drive is 40 minutes. A guest with no seat is the only seating failure anyone remembers, and it happens in front of both families.

So build to the guarantee plus four. That is 4% slack for $18 if you take it in table diameter rather than in a new table. If the seats go unused, they go unused quietly at the two tables nearest the bar.

Four Days, Four Deadlines

The chart is a document. The table count is a purchase order. They close on different days, and only one of them can be reopened.

Day What locks Cost of missing it
Mon, Nov 23 Caterer's count. The contract's five-business-day date was Friday the 20th Nothing today. After Wednesday, $3 a plate to change it
Tue, Nov 24 Rental manifest. Table and chair counts go on the truck A second delivery runs $75 to $125, if a truck exists
Wed, Nov 25, 2 p.m. Truck loads. Venue signs the floor plan and the fire marshal copy A folding table, 29 inches high against a 30-inch banquet table
Thu, Nov 26 Nothing. Warehouse dark, kitchen closed, office phones off Chairs can be moved by hand. Nothing else can
Fri, Nov 27, 8 a.m. Delivery window, usually a four-hour block, no counter open Self-haul: six chairs at a time in an SUV
Sat, Nov 28 Room set at 8 a.m., doors at 4, seating frozen Most venues bill a reset at $150 to $300

One decision makes the last three rows cheap. Print escort cards with names only, alphabetized by last name, and keep the table assignments on a separate card for the venue and the caterer. When a cousin appears at 3:40 with no seat, you move one card on the master list and hand them a name card that still works. Print table numbers on the escort cards and that late change means reprinting the whole alphabet at a copy shop on Black Friday.

What to Do in the Next 48 Hours

Four moves, in this order, and the whole thing takes about an hour.

Tonight, build the chart at the guarantee. Not at the RSVP list, not at the 120 you invited. 88 seats, eleven tables, and two of those tables upsized to 72 inches. Use the seating and table calculator for the room dimensions before you draw, because eleven rounds at 10 feet of footprint each is 1,100 square feet and a dance floor still has to fit.

Monday morning, call the rental company. Two 72-inch rounds instead of two 60s, and confirm the manifest for Tuesday. If they cannot swap, the answer is nine at two 60-inch rounds, not a twelfth table.

Monday afternoon, call the caterer and confirm 88. The contract date has passed, so this is a conversation, not a filing. Ask what a change to 90 costs after Wednesday. If the answer is $3 a plate, you now know the price of a late yes.

Tuesday, print. Name cards alphabetized, no table numbers, plus a master list for the venue. Skip the printed menus if the budget is tight and skip the chargers if the tables go to nine. The menu cost guide prices the printed pieces if you want them.

Then stop. The chart is done, the count is set, and the only thing left is the arrival of two people from Dallas who will be genuinely happy to see you.

Frequently Asked Questions

About $242 in fixed charges before any food: $42 for a 60-inch round and eight chairs, $24 for the floor-length linen, $11 for napkins, $16 for chargers, $121 for a delivered centerpiece, and $28 for place cards and menus. Add eight plates at $105 and the table runs $1,082. If only two guests force the table, those two cost $226 each, which is where the arithmetic stops feeling like a wedding budget.
Eight, and that is how caterers plan the room. A 60-inch round measures about 188 inches around the edge, which is 23.5 inches a person at eight and 20.9 at nine. You can seat nine by dropping chargers and shrinking the centerpiece, and the caterer will do it if you ask in advance. A 72-inch round seats ten, which is the number that matters on a holiday weekend when two more people always appear.
The chart itself can change until the night before if your escort cards carry names only and no table numbers. The table count is the number that locks, and on Thanksgiving week it locks Tuesday, November 24, when the rental company builds the truck manifest. After Wednesday at 2 p.m. the warehouse is dark until Monday, so a twelfth table becomes a folding table you buy and a linen that does not fit.

Upsize two tables to 72 inches for $18, seat 92, feed whoever shows up, and keep the master list current until the doors open at four. Couples who have a bad seating chart are rarely the ones who ran out of chairs. They are the ones who printed table numbers in October.
