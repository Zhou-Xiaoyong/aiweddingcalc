The dress is the one purchase every guest will see, and it's easy to under-budget. The average wedding dress cost in 2026 is about $1,600–$2,000 for the gown alone in the US, but the full look — dress plus alterations, veil, shoes, and accessories — usually lands closer to $2,500–$3,500. Put in context, the gown is a small slice of the average US wedding of roughly $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study), yet it's the line couples most often remember blowing. This guide breaks down the real numbers, what moves the price, and exactly where to save.

The Average Wedding Dress Cost in 2026

Across US brides, the typical spend breaks down like this in 2026:

  • Gown (new, salon): $1,600–$2,000 average; range $500–$10,000+.
  • Alterations: $200–$800.
  • Veil: $100–$400.
  • Shoes: $80–$250.
  • Jewelry, sash, undergarments: $150–$400.
  • Preservation/cleaning: $150–$300 (after the wedding).

So a bride shopping the average pays ~$1,800 for the dress and another ~$700–$1,200 to wear and finish it. The cheaper routes — off-the-rack, sample, and secondhand — can deliver a designer look for under $1,000 all-in.

What Drives the Price

Five factors explain most of the spread between a $600 gown and a $6,000 one:

  • Designer & brand. Established designers command a premium; in-house and indie labels cost less for similar construction.
  • Fabric & beading. Beaded lace, hand-appliqué, and silk satin cost far more than lined tulle or polyester mikado.
  • Silhouette & structure. Ballgowns and fit-and-flare styles with boning and multiple layers need more labor than a simple slip or A-line.
  • Customization. Made-to-order color, added sleeves, or a custom train adds cost and lead time.
  • Where you buy. Full-service salons bundle styling and alterations support into the price; online and warehouse retailers strip that out.

Dress Price Tiers

Use these brackets to set expectations before you walk in:

  • Budget ($500–$1,200): off-the-rack, department-store bridal lines, and many online retailers. Great for simple silhouettes.
  • Mid-range ($1,200–$2,500): the most common salon tier; quality fabrics and recognizable designers.
  • Designer ($2,500–$6,000): name-brand gowns with intricate detail and structured construction.
  • Couture / custom ($6,000+): made-to-measure with extensive handwork; typically 6–9 months lead time.

Lead time matters as much as price: designer and couture gowns need 4–9 months, while off-the-rack and secondhand can be yours in weeks.

Don't Forget the Add-Ons

The gown price is rarely the final number. Build these in from day one:

  • Alterations — almost every gown needs hemming, straps, and a bustle.
  • Veil & headpiece — $100–$400, more for cathedral length or comb detailing.
  • Shoes — comfortable mid-range ($80–$150) beats invisible designer heels.
  • Lingerie & shapewear — chosen to suit the neckline and fabric.
  • Jewelry & belt — borrowed or bought; $50–$200 keeps it reasonable.
  • Preservation — $150–$300 after the day if you want to keep the dress.

Where to Buy

Each channel trades price for service:

  • Bridal salons: best for try-on and expert styling; highest prices but often include alterations credit.
  • Department & bridal-chain stores: reliable off-the-rack at lower cost.
  • Online retailers: lowest prices, huge selection, but no in-person fitting — order early and size up.
  • Trunk shows: designer visits with 10–15% show discounts and first access to new styles.
  • Rental: wear a designer gown once for a fraction of retail — ideal for non-traditional venues.

Sample Sales & Secondhand

The fastest way to cut the average wedding dress cost in half:

  • Sample sales sell floor models 40–70% off; sizes run limited and the dress may need extra alterations.
  • Secondhand marketplaces list worn-once and new-with-tags gowns at steep discounts.
  • Buy-sell-trade groups let you grab this year's styles months after release.

Trade-offs: limited return windows, possible flaws, and alteration needs. But saving $1,500 on the dress can fund your venue or honeymoon instead. Run the trade-off in our wedding budget calculator before you decide.

Alterations: The Hidden Must-Budget Line

Alterations are where dress budgets quietly break. Budget $200–$800, more for structured gowns:

  • Hem: $80–$250 (layers and lace cost more).
  • Take-in / bustle: $100–$300.
  • Straps, cups, custom sizing: $50–$200 each.
  • Rush alterations (under 4 weeks): add 25–50%.

Book your seamstress when you buy the dress, not two weeks before the wedding. The dress budget calculator separates gown and alterations so this line never surprises you.

How to Save Without Sacrificing the Look

  • Shop off-season sample sales and trunk shows for 10–70% off.
  • Buy a size or two up secondhand and invest the savings in great alterations.
  • Skip the matching designer shoes — comfort reads better in photos than a label.
  • Borrow jewelry and a veil from married friends or family.
  • Choose a simpler silhouette in luxe fabric over heavy beading.
  • Set the dress number first, then fund the gap with the savings calculator so you're not pulling from catering.

Building the Dress Into Your Total Budget

The dress should sit at roughly 5–8% of your total wedding spend. On a $34,200 average wedding that's about $1,700–$2,700 for the full look — right in line with the real numbers above. If your dream gown pushes past that, shift savings from a lower-priority category (favors, send-off, upgraded linens) rather than the venue or food.
