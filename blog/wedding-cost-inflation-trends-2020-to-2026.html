If you got engaged in 2026, you're planning against a very different price level than couples a few years ago. After a COVID-era collapse, US wedding costs rocketed to a peak and have only recently leveled off. The average US wedding now costs about $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study) — up sharply from the ~$19,000 couples reported in 2020, yet close to the ~$35,000 peak of 2023. Understanding the wedding cost inflation trends from 2020 to 2026 isn't just trivia: it tells you whether prices are still climbing, which categories to watch, and how aggressively to save. This guide walks the year-by-year arc and the forces behind it.

The Big Picture: 2020 to 2026

Approximate average US wedding spend by year (The Knot Real Weddings figures for 2020–2023; 2024–2026 reflect the trend converging on the latest study):

  • 2020: ~$19,000 — tiny COVID weddings and mass postponements.
  • 2021: ~$28,000 — postponed weddings flood back.
  • 2022: ~$30,000 — demand and prices climb.
  • 2023: ~$35,000 — the recent peak.
  • 2024: ~$33,000 — early settling.
  • 2025: ~$34,000 — stabilizing.
  • 2026: ~$34,200 — current average (The Knot 2026).

That's roughly an 80% swing from the 2020 low to the 2023 high, then a gentle return toward the mid-$30,000s.

2020: The COVID Dip

2020 is the outlier. With gatherings limited, many couples postponed or held micro-weddings of 10–20 people. Average reported spend collapsed to about $19,000 — not because weddings got cheaper, but because guest counts and venues shrank overnight. The "savings" were an artifact of smaller events, not lower prices.

2021–2022: Rebound & Pent-Up Demand

Two years of postponed weddings hit the calendar at once. Vendor calendars filled, and couples who'd waited wanted to celebrate properly:

  • 2021 (~$28,000): the first wave of make-up weddings, still smaller-than-planned for many.
  • 2022 (~$30,000): full guest counts return and prices begin rising with broad inflation.

Scarcity let vendors raise rates, and supply-chain costs pushed flowers, rentals, and catering higher.

2023: The Peak

By 2023 the average reached about $35,000 — the high-water mark of the decade. Post-COVID premiumization (bigger guest lists, upgraded photography and decor) met peak vendor demand and elevated food and venue costs. For couples planning then, budgets stretched fastest in venue, catering, and floral.

2024–2026: Settling Near $34,200

The sharp climb has flattened. 2024 eased to ~$33,000 as some couples right-sized their plans, 2025 held near ~$34,000, and 2026 sits at about $34,200 per the latest study. Costs now move with general inflation rather than the dramatic post-COVID jump — which makes 2026 budgeting far more predictable than 2021–2023 was. Why Wedding Costs Inflated

Four forces stacked on top of each other:

  • Broad post-2021 inflation. Venue, food, and flower prices rose with the general economy.
  • Pent-up demand & scarcity. Postponed 2020 weddings flooded 2021–2022, filling vendor calendars and lifting rates.
  • Wage growth. Photographers, planners, and caterers raised prices as labor costs rose.
  • Premiumization. After waiting, couples upgraded experiences — bigger guest counts, better vendors, more decor.

Which Categories Rose Fastest

  • Venue & catering: the biggest line (40–50% of budget) and the fastest climber with food costs.
  • Florals: supply-chain and import pressures pushed per-arrangement prices up sharply.
  • Photography & videography: experienced pros raised rates as demand outstripped supply.
  • Decor & rentals: the "experience" upgrade showed up here most.

Weddings vs. General Inflation

Wedding costs outran general CPI during 2021–2023 because demand spiked and prices rose — a double whammy unique to a postponed-wedding backlog. Since 2024, wedding inflation has tracked closer to overall CPI, so annual increases are now modest (a few percent) rather than double-digit. The practical takeaway: expect small yearly drift, not another 2023-style leap.

What It Means for 2026 Couples

  • Prices are stable, not falling. Don't wait for a drop — book when your date is set.
  • Location dominates. At ~$34,200 national, your state can swing you $22,000–$50,000.
  • Vendor rates are firm. The era of easy negotiation (2020) is long gone; lock deposits early.
  • Plan at today's level. Use a 2026 estimator, not a 2022 memory.

How to Beat the Trend

  • Off-peak month or weekday: 20–30% venue savings alone offsets a year of inflation.
  • Shrink the guest list: venue + catering scale directly with heads.
  • Book vendors from a cheaper nearby market if you're near a high-cost city.
  • Start a dedicated savings plan with the savings calculator so rises don't surprise you.
  • Watch catering & florals — the categories that inflated fastest; our catering calculator and flower calculator keep them honest.
  • Consider a planner only where it pays back — the planner cost calculator shows the trade-off.
