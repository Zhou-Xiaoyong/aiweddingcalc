A Tampa venue quotes $78 a head for a buffet, adds a 22% service charge and county sales tax, and a 120-guest wedding lands at $31,400. The average wedding cost in Florida is $33,000 in 2026, with most couples spending $24,000 to $55,000. The national average is $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study), so the state as a whole sits about $1,200 under the line.

That number hides the thing you actually need to know. Florida is 800 miles long, and the difference between a Miami Beach ballroom and a barn outside Ocala is not a matter of taste. It is roughly $25,000. Your budget gets set by which market you pick and which month you pick inside it, in that order.

Florida Wedding Cost at a Glance

The headline picture for 2026, before the city detail:

  • Statewide average: ~$33,000
  • Typical range: $24,000 – $55,000
  • National average: ~$34,200, so Florida is about $1,200 under
  • Tier: Near Average, pulled up by four coastal destination markets
  • All-in cost per guest: about $280 at the typical 117-guest wedding
  • Cheapest months: August and September, 20% to 35% below peak venue rates
  • Most expensive months: January through April
  • Priciest markets: Miami Beach, Key West, Palm Beach, Naples
  • Most affordable: inland North Florida, Ocala, Gainesville, and the inland Panhandle

Read the calendar lines twice, because they run opposite to the rest of the country. Florida's cheap months are the ones with heat advisories, and its expensive months are the ones most states call off-season. That inversion drives nearly every decision here, and it is why a cheapest month to get married list built for the national market will steer you wrong.

The coastal premium is not gradual. Crossing from Tampa to Miami Beach roughly doubles the venue line, because a hotel that can sell a February Saturday to a corporate group has no reason to discount it for you. The cheapest Florida weddings are not on the water at all: springs, ranches, and small-town halls an hour inland rent for a tenth of a beachfront site fee and often allow outside catering.

Why the Average Wedding Cost in Florida Swings With the Calendar

A venue's rate card in Florida has almost nothing to do with the national seasonal pattern. Here is what the 2026 pricing actually looks like, expressed as a change against the venue's own annual average:

When Venue Rate vs. Annual Average The Catch
January – April+15% to +25%Dry season and the best weather of the year, which is why everyone wants it
May−10%Hot afternoons, but the rain pattern has not fully arrived. The best value of the dry half-year
June – August−20% to −35%Heat index regularly clears 95°F; hurricane season opened June 1
September−25% to −35%Cheapest month in the state, and the statistical peak of the Atlantic season lands around September 10
October – November−5% to −15%Humidity drops, rates stay soft. The window most Florida planners book for themselves
December+10%, and +25% over the holidaysHotel rates spike for the holidays, which inflates every room block and out-of-town guest

The dollar version of that table: a Miami Beach hotel that quotes a $25,000 food-and-beverage minimum for a February Saturday is often at $12,000 to $14,000 for the same room in August. That is not a coupon. It is the same 120 guests at the same tables, with the thermostat reading 94.

Day of week matters just as much. A Saturday carries a 20% to 30% premium over a Friday or Sunday almost everywhere in the state, and Sunday brunch is the deepest discount available at any country club or hotel that serves it. If you want peak-season weather without the peak-season invoice, a Sunday in April usually beats a Saturday in May on both price and comfort.

Hurricane Season and the Clause That Saves Your Deposit

The Atlantic season runs June 1 through November 30. That is half the year, and it overlaps every discounted date on the table above. This is not a reason to avoid a Florida wedding. It is a reason to read one paragraph of your contract before you sign it.

Deposits at Florida venues typically run 25% to 50% of the estimated total, and they are usually non-refundable. What saves you is the force majeure clause, sometimes called a named-storm clause. A workable version says that if a named storm triggers an evacuation order or a government closure in your county, you may reschedule within 12 months without paying a second site fee. A useless version says the venue will "work with you," which means nothing on the day a storm is 48 hours out and the venue has four other couples asking the same question.

Two details decide whether the clause is worth the paper. First, reschedules are almost always priced at the new year's rates, so a 2027 date may cost 8% to 15% more than the 2026 contract you signed. Second, check whether the service charge and tax come back with the site fee, since on a $30,000 wedding that is another $6,000 sitting in the balance.

Cancellation insurance is the backstop. Liability-only coverage for a $1 million event runs about $75 to $300, while cancellation coverage usually costs 1% to 2% of your total budget, so roughly $330 to $660 on a $33,000 wedding. Buy it months ahead. A policy will not cover a storm that already has a name, and many carriers require the policy to be in force well before the system forms. Standard homeowners policies exclude flooding, so a separate rider is the only thing that pays if the reception hall takes on water. Six Florida Charges That Aren't in the Proposal

Every one of these has shown up on a real Florida invoice after the couple thought the number was final.

  • The service charge is not the tip. Florida venues and caterers add 20% to 24%, and only part of it reaches the staff. Ask directly what percentage is distributed to servers and bartenders, then plan a separate cash tip if the answer is thin. Our wedding vendor tipping guide has the per-person amounts.
  • Florida taxes the service charge. A mandatory service charge is treated as part of the taxable sale here, so that 22% gets taxed too. On the Tampa budget above, that is about $150 you did not see in the quote.
  • Resort fees and valet. Hotel venues commonly charge $25 to $45 per room per night in resort fees and $8 to $15 per car for valet. On a 30-room block over two nights, that is $1,800 to $2,700 that never appears in the wedding proposal because it lands on your guests.
  • Vendor meals at resort properties. Expect $45 to $75 per vendor plate, and four to six vendors eating. A $300 line you forgot to budget becomes $350 with tax and service.
  • Beach permits and logistics. A public beach ceremony permit is often $50 to $500, but you also pay delivery and setup for chairs and an arch at a site with thin parking, and some towns require a certificate of insurance naming the municipality. Budget $800 to $2,000 for a barefoot ceremony that looked free online.
  • Room block attrition. Contracts usually guarantee 80% pickup. Florida guests book condos and vacation rentals instead of the block, so the shortfall gets billed to you at the group rate. Read that clause and negotiate it down to 70% before you sign.

Five Ways to Cut a Florida Wedding

1. Take May or November

May runs about 10% under a venue's annual average and November sits 5% to 15% under, with weather that is still workable outdoors. Both beat fighting for a February Saturday, and both keep you out of the September storm peak.

2. Move 40 minutes inland

Sarasota to Myakka, Miami to the Redland, Orlando to Mount Dora. Site fees drop 30% to 50% and caterers stop quoting beach-season prices.

3. Trade Saturday dinner for Sunday brunch

Brunch catering runs $45 to $65 per person against $95 for a plated dinner, and the bar bill falls from roughly $25 a head to $10 because nobody drinks like it is 8 p.m. On 120 guests, that combination typically saves $4,000 to $6,000.

4. Buy out a restaurant instead of renting a room

You skip the site fee, get furniture, staff, and a working kitchen, and usually negotiate the minimum down for a Sunday. The trade is a guest cap, typically 80 to 120, and less control over the room.

5. Cut 20 guests

At $262 a head in Tampa, 20 guests is $5,240. That is a better return than any negotiation on flowers, and it is the one line where the savings compound across catering, bar, rentals, cake, and favors at once. Run the tradeoff through the guest list calculator before you send save-the-dates.
