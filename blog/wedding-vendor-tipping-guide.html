You've budgeted for the venue, the caterer, the photographer, and the band. But there's one line item that surprises almost every couple in the final week: gratuities. Wedding tips are almost always paid in cash, on the day, and they can easily add up to $2,000 or more.

This guide gives you exact, etiquette-approved amounts for every vendor — plus the vendors you can skip without guilt.

How Much to Budget for Tips

Plan to set aside 5–10% of your total wedding budget for gratuities. With the average US wedding now costing about $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study), that means $1,700–$3,400 in cash tips for a typical celebration.

The exact number depends on your vendor lineup, your region, and how many staff work your reception. Larger guest counts mean more servers, more bartenders, and bigger total tips.

The Complete Tipping Cheat Sheet

Here are the standard 2026 gratuity ranges used by wedding planners across the US:

  • Catering staff / servers: 15–20% of the food bill (if not already included), or $25–$50 per server
  • Bartenders: 10–15% of the total bar bill, split among the team ($25–$50 each minimum)
  • Hair stylist & makeup artist: 15–25%, exactly like a salon visit
  • Delivery & setup crews (flowers, cake, rentals): $5–$20 per person
  • Ceremony musicians: $15–$25 per musician
  • DJ: $50–$150, or 10–15% of the contract
  • Live band: $25–$50 per band member
  • Photographer / videographer (non-owner): $50–$200 each
  • Second shooters & assistants: $50–$100 each
  • Drivers (limo, shuttle): 15–20% of the transportation bill — check if it's already on the contract
  • Officiant (civil): $50–$100
  • Clergy: a $100–$500 donation to the house of worship is customary instead of a personal tip
  • Wedding planner: optional — 10–20% or a thoughtful gift for exceptional service
  • Venue coordinator / banquet captain: $100–$300 if they went above and beyond
  • Restroom / coat-check attendants: $1–$2 per guest, often pre-arranged

Vendors You Should Always Tip

These vendors are service workers whose compensation assumes gratuity, so treat their tips as a required budget line:

  • Catering and bar staff — unless your contract explicitly states gratuity is included and distributed to staff
  • Hair and makeup artists — standard salon etiquette applies on wedding day
  • Drivers — 15–20% is the industry norm, sometimes auto-added
  • Delivery and setup crews — small cash tips at drop-off keep the day running smoothly

Vendors Where Tips Are Optional (But Appreciated)

For skilled professionals who set their own pricing, a tip is a bonus for excellence, not an obligation:

  • Photographers and videographers — tip staff shooters; owners don't expect it
  • DJs and bands — tip for a packed dance floor and smooth MC work
  • Planners and coordinators — a heartfelt review and referral is often valued as much as cash
  • Florists and bakers — tip the delivery/setup crew rather than the owner

Who You Do NOT Need to Tip

  • Business owners — the traditional rule: owners set their prices, employees earn tips
  • Venue staff already covered by a distributed service charge — verify in writing
  • Vendors with gratuity written into the contract — don't double-tip; read the fine print

Service Charges vs. Gratuities — Read This Twice

This is where most couples overpay or underpay. A "service charge" (typically 18–22% on catering) is not automatically a tip. In many venues it covers administrative overhead, insurance, and staffing logistics — and never reaches your servers.

Ask your caterer one question: "Is the service charge distributed to the service staff as gratuity?" If the answer is no (or vague), budget separate cash tips for servers and bartenders.

Also note: in most states, sales tax applies to the service charge too, which inflates your final invoice beyond the menu math. Our Tax & Tip Calculator models this exact stack — subtotal, tax, service charge, and tips.

Tipping Logistics: Envelopes & Timing

Three days before the wedding:

  1. Withdraw cash and prepare one labeled envelope per vendor (write the amount on a sticky note inside the flap so your helper can verify).
  2. Assign a distributor — best man, day-of coordinator, or a parent. Couples should not be handing out envelopes mid-reception.
  3. Time it right: hair/makeup tips in the morning at service, ceremony musician and officiant tips right after the ceremony, and catering/bar/DJ tips at the end of the night.

Pro tip: prepare two or three blank extra envelopes with $20–$50 for surprise MVPs — the server who saved the cake table, or the driver who made an extra run.
