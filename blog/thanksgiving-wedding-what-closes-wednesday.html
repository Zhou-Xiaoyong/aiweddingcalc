The florist's order desk stops taking Friday deliveries at noon on Wednesday, November 25. The rental will-call window shuts at 5. The county clerk locks up at 4:30 with your marriage license still on her desk, and the last UPS truck of the week pulls out of the depot at 6.

Thanksgiving is Thursday. Almost nothing that a wedding depends on opens again until Friday morning, and a few things never open at all. This is the week where the wedding stops being a planning problem and becomes a logistics problem with a hard cutoff.

The six items a couple usually still needs on Wednesday cost $910.48. The same six items bought on Friday cost $1,322.48. That gap is $412, which is $4.68 a guest on an 88-person Thanksgiving wedding and 1.2% of the $34,200 US average. Money you can find. The problem is the item that isn't on the list because it can't be bought at any price on Friday.

The Wednesday Cliff Every Thanksgiving Wedding Hits

Thanksgiving falls on a Thursday, which makes Wednesday the last business day of the week for anything that needs a person behind a counter. Friday doesn't rescue it. The day after Thanksgiving is a holiday on many government and business calendars, and the offices that do open spend Friday working through two days of backlog with a Saturday wedding in the queue ahead of yours.

Here is the order things close, and what each missed deadline turns into.

None of this is a price increase. The chairs still rent for $9 each and the ice still sells for $2.79 a bag. The $150 is a second truck. Rental companies run deliveries on a route with a flat minimum per stop, so a Friday request for ten chairs doesn't cost ten chairs. It costs ten chairs, a new stop, and a driver who has to be on the road at 6 a.m. the morning of your wedding instead of loading the truck for the venue at the venue.

The $64 on the cake is the same mechanism. Friday is the busiest pickup day of the month for any bakery, and a cake that was scheduled to be baked Wednesday gets squeezed in as a rush ticket or doesn't get baked. Ask the shop one question on Wednesday: is my cake baked before Thursday? If the answer is "Friday morning," you have a $64 line you haven't seen yet.

The florist line is the one people misread. Two centerpieces rebuilt on Friday aren't expensive because flowers got more expensive. They're expensive because wholesale is closed, so you're buying at retail florist prices, $85 a piece instead of $55, and a retail shop on the Friday after Thanksgiving is doing twelve deliveries before yours.

Three Things the Holiday Takes and Doesn't Give Back

The $412 is the cheap part. These are the three things where the holiday wins outright.

  • The marriage license. The clerk closes Wednesday afternoon, and the day after Thanksgiving is a holiday in a lot of courthouses. A license collected Monday or Tuesday is the whole answer, and a license not collected by 4:30 on Wednesday is a wedding that isn't legal on Saturday. Our license deadline guide for Thanksgiving weekend works the waiting periods backward for the states that have them, and the officiant cost calculator prices the person who signs it.
  • Cash. A $34,200 wedding carries roughly $1,700 to $3,400 in cash gratuities, and banks are shut Thursday. An ATM caps a card at $300 to $500 a day, so a couple starting from zero on Wednesday morning cannot physically withdraw that much before the ceremony. Our tipping guide breaks the total down by vendor so you know which envelopes need to be fat.
  • Liquor. Texas and Massachusetts close package stores by law on Thanksgiving, and Pennsylvania's state stores stay shut. If you're supplying your own bar, Wednesday is the last day to buy, full stop. The alcohol calculator sizes the order, and the bar shopping list is the one to take to the store.

Everything else on the closure list is money. These three are capability. That's why the order below puts cash before chairs, even though the chairs are on this week's invoice and the cash isn't.

The Order to Spend It In

If you have one afternoon and about $900, spend it in this sequence. The first item on the list is the one that can't be fixed on Friday, and the rest is sorted by how much a guest notices.

  • License, before anything else. One stop, 20 minutes if the office is quiet, and the only line on this list that ends the wedding rather than adding to it.
  • Cash, second. Take the ATM withdrawal on Wednesday and keep it somewhere that isn't the wedding bag. Vendors who get a clean envelope at 9 p.m. remember it. Vendors who get a promise don't.
  • Seats, third. Ten chairs at $285 on Friday is worse than $135 on Wednesday, and still better than seating eight people at a table built for ten. Buy the seats, skip the second centerpiece.
  • Centerpieces, fourth. Nobody photographs the third centerpiece on the left. A grocery store floral department sells a $18 bouquet on Friday morning that reads fine at 20 feet, and two of those are $36 against the $170 rebuild.
  • Cake and hem, last. A sheet cake from the grocery store runs about $3.50 a slice and cuts fine. A hem can be hem tape and a hotel iron at 11 p.m. Neither is worth the $144 the two rush fees add up to.

The number to hold is $412, and the deadline is 5 p.m. Wednesday. You can beat most of it with a car and two hours: the ice, the chairs, the hand warmers, and the cash are all one strip-mall loop if you start before noon. What you can't beat is the clerk's 4:30 close or a bank that's shut on Thursday.
