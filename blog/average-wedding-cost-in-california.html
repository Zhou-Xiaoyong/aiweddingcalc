The average wedding cost in California is $43,000 in 2026 — about 26% above the $34,200 national average from The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study. That single figure hides an enormous spread. A Sonoma vineyard wedding and a Riverside County ranch wedding are both "California weddings," and there is often $30,000 between them.

What follows is the number broken apart in the three ways that actually change your planning: by region, by line item, and by guest count. We also cover the fees that exist only in California — the confidential marriage license, sales tax on service charges, coastal permits, and fire-season restrictions — because those are the ones that show up late and unbudgeted.

How Much Is the Average Wedding Cost in California in 2026?

California averages $43,000, placing it in the most expensive tier of US states alongside New York, New Jersey, Massachusetts, and Hawaii. The typical range runs $28,000 to $65,000, and wine country or luxury coastal celebrations regularly clear $70,000.

Three details matter more than the headline:

  • The average reflects about 100–125 guests. Statewide averages are not a price tag for your wedding; they are a price tag for someone else's guest list. Yours will move sharply in either direction.
  • It is weighted toward the coast. Most California weddings happen in the Bay Area, Los Angeles, Orange County, and San Diego, so the statewide figure sits far closer to coastal pricing than to Central Valley pricing.
  • It excludes the honeymoon and the rings. As with the national figure, engagement rings, wedding bands, and travel sit outside the number.

Average Wedding Cost in California by Region

California is really six wedding markets wearing one state's name. Here is what couples pay in each, assuming a comparable 100-guest semi-formal wedding:

  • Napa & Sonoma wine country — $58,000–$85,000. The most expensive wedding market in the state. Estate venues run buyout-style pricing, most require an approved caterer from a short list, and peak harvest weekends in September and October carry a premium on top of the premium.
  • San Francisco Bay Area — $52,000. Venue and labor costs are the highest in the state outside wine country. Catering alone commonly runs $135–$185 per person before tax and service charge.
  • Los Angeles & Orange County — $46,000. Enormous vendor supply keeps mid-tier pricing more competitive than the Bay Area, but venue minimums in Malibu, Pasadena, and Newport Beach match it.
  • San Diego — $40,000. Slightly below the state average, with strong value in East County and inland North County compared with coastal La Jolla and Coronado.
  • Sacramento & the foothills — $34,000. Almost exactly the national average, with real access to the same vendor pool that serves wine country an hour west.
  • Inland Empire, Central Valley & far north — $26,000–$32,000. Riverside, San Bernardino, Fresno, Bakersfield, and Redding price 30–40% below the Bay Area for the same guest count and formality.

The practical takeaway: a 60- to 90-minute drive inland from Los Angeles or the Bay Area typically saves $12,000 to $18,000 on an otherwise identical wedding. That is the single largest geographic lever available to California couples, and it costs you nothing but a shuttle line item.

The Full Line-Item Breakdown of the Average Wedding Cost in California

Here is where $43,000 goes for a 110-guest California wedding at mid-market 2026 rates. Percentages are of the total.

  • Venue rental + food & beverage minimum — $16,500 (38%). The dominant line item, and the one that sets the ceiling for everything else.
  • Catering above the minimum, bar, and service — $5,800 (13%). Coastal California catering runs $110–$165 per person; a 20–24% service charge and county sales tax stack on top.
  • Photography — $5,200 (12%). Eight hours with a second shooter. Bay Area and LA photographers with five-plus years of experience start near $4,500.
  • Flowers & decor — $4,300 (10%). Above the $2,800 national average, driven by labor rather than stems.
  • Attire, alterations, hair & makeup — $3,400 (8%). California alterations pricing runs 25–40% above the national norm.
  • Entertainment — $2,600 (6%). A DJ costs $1,800–$3,000; a live band moves this line to $7,000–$14,000.
  • Planner or coordinator — $2,200 (5%). Day-of coordination runs $1,500–$3,000; full-service planning runs 12–18% of total budget.
  • Videography — $1,600 (4%). Only about half of California couples book it; those who do spend $2,800–$5,000.
  • Cake, stationery, favors & transportation — $1,400 (3%).

Two structural notes. First, venue and catering together are half your budget — every meaningful saving starts there and almost nowhere else. Second, the service charge is not a tip. A 22% service charge on a $20,000 catering bill is $4,400 that goes to the venue, and in California it is generally taxable. Why California Weddings Cost 26% More Than the National Average

Four forces, in order of impact:

Venue real estate. A wedding venue is a commercial property with an event license, and California commercial rents are among the highest in the country. That cost is passed to you as a rental fee or, more commonly, as a food and beverage minimum. Bay Area and wine country properties routinely set Saturday minimums of $25,000–$45,000 in peak season — a number that eliminates most budgets before the conversation starts.

Labor. California's minimum wage, overtime rules, and worker classification requirements raise the cost of every staffed hour: catering servers, bartenders, floral assistants, load-in crews, and second shooters. This is why California flowers cost more than national average even when the stems come from the same growers — the stems are cheap in California, the hands arranging them are not.

Permits and compliance. Public parks, beaches, and state properties all require permits, and many require a certificate of insurance naming the agency as additionally insured. Amplified sound, alcohol service, and open flame each frequently need separate approval.

Demand concentration. Roughly two-thirds of California weddings target the same 20 peak Saturdays between May and October in the same handful of coastal counties. Vendors price accordingly.

What Guest Count Does to a California Wedding Budget

Every California wedding budget resolves to the same two-part formula: about $19,000 in fixed costs that do not move with headcount — photography, attire, planning, entertainment, officiant, license — plus roughly $215 per guest for catering, bar, rentals, place settings, and stationery.

  • 50 guests — about $29,750. Intimate, and the tier where a restaurant buyout or a boutique venue becomes realistic.
  • 75 guests — about $35,125. Roughly the national average, spent in California.
  • 100 guests — about $40,500. Just under the state average; this is the most common California wedding.
  • 150 guests — about $51,250. Now competing for the larger venues, which are also the pricier ones.
  • 200 guests — about $62,000. Above this, venue options narrow sharply in coastal counties and per-guest costs start rising rather than flattening.

The number to remember is $215 per name. A guest list that grows by 25 people costs about $5,400 — the difference between a DJ and a band, or between six and ten hours of photography. California-Only Costs Couples Forget to Budget

These do not appear in national wedding cost guides, and they catch California couples late:

  • Marriage license: $35–$115, and there are two kinds. A public license requires one witness at the ceremony and becomes public record. A confidential license — unique to California — requires that you already live together, needs no witness, and is not publicly searchable. Neither has a waiting period, and both are valid for 90 days. Fees are set by county, so Los Angeles, Alameda, and San Diego all differ.
  • Sales tax on rentals and service charges: 7.25%–10.75%. California taxes tangible rentals, and mandatory service charges on catering are generally taxable too. On a $25,000 food, beverage, and rental spend, that is $2,000–$2,700 that many couples never wrote down.
  • Coastal and park permits: $150–$1,500. Beach ceremonies in coastal jurisdictions, state parks, and many city parks require permits, insurance, and sometimes a refundable cleaning deposit. Peak-season slots at popular beaches book 9–12 months out.
  • Fire season restrictions: variable, but plan for it. Many California counties prohibit sparkler send-offs, open-flame candles, and outdoor lighting rigs during declared fire season, which typically spans late summer into fall. Wine country venues may also carry evacuation or air-quality clauses. Read the force majeure language in your contract before you book an autumn date.
  • Guest travel and hotel blocks. California's size works against you. Guests flying into SFO for a Sonoma wedding or LAX for a Santa Barbara wedding need transportation, and shuttle service for 100 guests runs $1,200–$2,800.

How to Cut $10,000–$15,000 Off a California Wedding

Ranked by dollars saved per unit of sacrifice:

  1. Move off a peak Saturday — saves 20–35% of venue cost, or $3,000–$7,000. Friday evenings and Sundays carry the same daylight, the same vendors, and a materially lower minimum. Sunday of a three-day holiday weekend is the best-value date in California.
  2. Marry in the off-season — saves $4,000–$8,000. January, February, and early March are genuinely slow statewide. Southern California's advantage is that the weather stays cooperative, so an off-season date costs you far less comfort here than it would in the Northeast.
  3. Cut the guest list — saves $215 per name. Twenty fewer guests is $4,300, and it is the only lever that improves every category simultaneously.
  4. Choose a venue that allows outside catering — saves $3,000–$9,000. Escaping an in-house food and beverage minimum is usually worth more than any negotiation you will win on the minimum itself. Compare formats with the wedding venue cost calculator.
  5. Go inland — saves $12,000–$18,000. The largest lever of all, if you are open to it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average wedding cost in California in 2026?

About $43,000 — roughly 26% above the $34,200 national average. Most California weddings fall between $28,000 and $65,000, and the statewide figure assumes about 100–125 guests.

Which part of California is cheapest to get married in?

The Inland Empire, Central Valley, and far north, at $26,000–$32,000. Sacramento sits mid-state at about $34,000. Driving 60–90 minutes inland from a coastal metro typically saves $12,000–$18,000.

How much does a wedding venue cost in California?

Rental plus food and beverage minimum runs $15,000–$22,000, about 38% of the budget. Bay Area and wine country Saturday minimums reach $25,000–$45,000; inland venues run $4,000–$10,000.

How much is a marriage license in California?

$35–$115 depending on county. California offers a public license (one witness required, public record) and a confidential license (cohabitation required, no witness, not public). No waiting period; valid 90 days.

How many guests does the average California wedding have?

About 100–125. Budget math is roughly $19,000 fixed plus $215 per guest, which puts 50 guests near $29,750 and 200 guests near $62,000.

How can you save money on a California wedding?

Shift off a peak Saturday, marry January through early March, cut the guest list, and choose a venue without an in-house food and beverage minimum. Together those four typically save $10,000–$15,000.
