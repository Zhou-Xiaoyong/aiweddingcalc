The average wedding cost in New York City is about $63,000 in 2026 — roughly 84% above the $34,200 US average reported in The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study, and well above New York State's $50,000 average, which upstate counties pull down. Most couples marrying in the five boroughs spend between $45,000 and $95,000.

That headline number is close to useless on its own, though. A Tribeca loft wedding and a Bay Ridge catering hall wedding are both "NYC weddings," and there is often $35,000 between them. Below, the figure is broken apart the three ways that actually change your decisions — by borough, by line item, and by guest count — plus the New York–specific fees that tend to surface after the contracts are signed.

How Much Is the Average Wedding Cost in New York City in 2026?

Citywide, plan on $63,000. New York is the most expensive state in the country at about $50,000, and the five boroughs sit meaningfully above that state line — Buffalo, Rochester, and the Southern Tier are what keep the statewide figure from being higher. Our state-by-state wedding cost comparison shows where New York ranks against the other 49.

Three caveats before you anchor on $63,000:

  • It assumes about 120–125 guests. NYC guest lists skew slightly smaller than the national norm because venue capacity is genuinely constrained, but the per-guest cost is far higher — so the total still climbs.
  • It excludes rings, honeymoon, and rehearsal dinner. Same convention as the national figure. In New York, a rehearsal dinner for 30 at a decent restaurant is $3,000–$6,000 on its own.
  • It is a median-ish average, not a floor. Real NYC weddings happen at $18,000 and at $250,000. The distribution here is wider than in almost any other US market.

For comparison, the other big-ticket coastal market — covered in our breakdown of the average wedding cost in California — runs about $43,000 statewide. New York City is roughly $20,000 more expensive than the entire state of California, which tells you most of what you need to know about the market you're planning in.

Average Wedding Cost in New York City by Borough

The single largest variable in NYC wedding pricing is not your vendor taste level. It's your subway stop. Here's what a comparable 125-guest semi-formal Saturday wedding costs in each borough:

  • Manhattan — about $74,000. The most expensive wedding submarket in the United States. Hotel ballrooms and event lofts below 96th Street set Saturday food and beverage minimums of $20,000–$45,000, and many buildings add loading dock, freight elevator, and overtime charges on top.
  • Brooklyn — about $60,000. Industrial waterfront venues in DUMBO, Williamsburg, and Greenpoint command Manhattan-adjacent pricing for the skyline view. Move inland to Prospect Heights, Crown Heights, or Bay Ridge and the same guest count runs $15,000 less.
  • Queens — about $48,000. The best value-per-dollar in the city. Long Island City has the skyline venues; Astoria, Flushing, and Forest Hills have deep, competitive catering-hall inventory where all-inclusive packages run $150–$225 per person.
  • The Bronx — about $42,000. Country clubs, City Island waterfront restaurants, and the New York Botanical Garden anchor the range. Vendor travel fees start appearing if your team is based in Brooklyn.
  • Staten Island — about $40,000. The most affordable borough, with a strong tradition of full-service catering halls that bundle food, bar, cake, and décor into one per-person price.

The practical arithmetic: moving a 125-guest wedding from Manhattan to Queens saves about $26,000 — the same wedding, the same vendors in many cases, one bridge or tunnel away. That is the largest single lever available to New York couples, and it costs you nothing but a paragraph on the invitation about the 7 train.

Where a $63,000 NYC Budget Actually Goes

Here is the line-item split for a 125-guest New York City wedding at mid-market 2026 rates:

  • Venue rental + food and beverage minimum — $27,000 (43%). Higher share of the total than in any other US market. This line sets the ceiling on everything else.
  • Catering above the minimum, bar, service charge — $8,000 (13%). NYC plated dinners run $145–$225 per person before the 22–24% service charge and 8.875% sales tax.
  • Photography — $6,300 (10%). Eight hours with a second shooter. Established NYC photographers start near $5,500 and clear $10,000 at the top of the mid-market.
  • Flowers and décor — $6,000 (9%). More than double the $2,800 national average, driven almost entirely by design labor and delivery logistics rather than stems.
  • Attire, alterations, hair and makeup — $4,400 (7%). Manhattan alterations and on-site glam teams price 30–50% above the national norm.
  • Entertainment — $3,800 (6%). A solid NYC DJ runs $2,000–$3,500; a live band moves this line to $8,000–$18,000.
  • Planner or coordinator — $3,200 (5%). Day-of coordination is $2,000–$4,000 here; full-service planning runs 12–18% of total budget and is close to mandatory for a raw-space wedding.
  • Videography — $2,300 (4%). Roughly half of NYC couples book it; those who do spend $3,500–$7,000.
  • Cake, stationery, favors, transportation — $2,000 (3%).

Two structural notes. First, venue and catering together are 56% of the budget — real savings begin there and essentially nowhere else. Second, the service charge is not a tip: 23% on a $30,000 catering bill is $6,900 that goes to the venue, and in New York the mandatory portion is generally taxable too. Model your own per-person math with the wedding catering calculator, then stress-test the final invoice with the wedding tax and tip calculator before you sign. Couples routinely underestimate this stack by $5,000.

Why New York City Weddings Cost 84% More Than the National Average

Four forces, in order of impact:

Real estate math. Every wedding venue in New York is a commercial property competing against tenants who would pay rent 365 days a year. The venue recovers that opportunity cost through your food and beverage minimum, which is why Manhattan minimums eliminate most budgets before the tour even happens.

Labor rates. Servers, bartenders, load-in crews, floral assistants, and second shooters all cost more per hour here than anywhere else in the country. This is the real reason NYC flowers cost double the national average — the stems ship from the same growers; the hands arranging them at 6 a.m. in a Chelsea studio do not.

Building friction. This is the NYC-specific one, and it is invisible until it isn't. Loading dock access windows, freight elevator operators at overtime rates, certificates of insurance naming the building as additionally insured, union labor requirements at some hotels and convention properties, and hard curfews that trigger overtime after midnight. Budget $1,000–$4,000 for this category at a raw-space venue.

Tax on top of a service charge. The combined sales tax rate in the five boroughs is 8.875%, and it generally applies to the mandatory service charge as well as the food, beverage, and rentals. On a $35,000 food-beverage-rental spend, that's about $3,100 that never appears in the proposal's headline number.

What Guest Count Does to a New York City Wedding Budget

Every NYC wedding budget reduces to the same two-part formula: roughly $26,000 in fixed costs that don't move with headcount — photography, attire, planning, entertainment, video, license — plus about $300 per guest for catering, bar, rentals, place settings, and stationery.

  • 50 guests — about $41,000. The tier where a restaurant buyout becomes not just possible but often the smartest format in the city.
  • 75 guests — about $48,500. Still workable in a single private dining room or a small loft.
  • 100 guests — about $56,000. The most common NYC wedding size.
  • 125 guests — about $63,500. The citywide average, and the point where Manhattan venue minimums stop being the binding constraint.
  • 150 guests — about $71,000. Now competing for the larger ballrooms, which are also the pricier ones.
  • 200 guests — about $86,000. Above this, five-borough venue options narrow sharply and per-guest costs start rising rather than flattening.

The number to internalize is $300 per name — the highest per-guest figure of any US market. Twenty extra guests costs $6,000, which in NYC terms is the entire difference between a DJ and a 10-piece band. Price your list in tiers with the wedding guest list calculator before a single save-the-date goes out, because trimming later is a social problem, not a spreadsheet problem.

NYC-Only Costs Couples Forget to Budget

These don't appear in national wedding cost guides, and they're the ones that show up at week 40:

  • Marriage license: $35, with a 24-hour waiting period. Apply online through the NYC City Clerk's Project Cupid system, then attend an in-person appointment to collect it. You cannot legally marry until 24 hours after issuance, and the license expires in 60 days — so don't pick it up too early. A civil ceremony at the Marriage Bureau on Worth Street adds $25.
  • Parks and public-space permits: $25 application plus site fees. Central Park, Prospect Park, Brooklyn Bridge Park, and most city green space require a special-event permit for anything beyond a tiny gathering. Popular ceremony spots (Cop Cot, the Ladies' Pavilion, Wagner Cove) book out 6–9 months ahead for peak Saturdays.
  • Building and logistics fees: $1,000–$4,000. Loading dock reservations, freight elevator operators, COI processing, security guards required by the certificate of occupancy, and after-midnight overtime. Ask for this list in writing before you sign a raw-space contract.
  • Guest transportation and hotel blocks. Nobody drives. Shuttles between a ceremony and reception across boroughs run $1,200–$3,000, and NYC hotel blocks are $250–$450 per night in peak season — a real factor in whether guests attend. Price the movement piece with the wedding transportation cost calculator.
  • Vendor travel and parking. Many NYC vendors add travel fees for cross-borough work, and almost all pass through parking or garage costs. It's small money that appears on five invoices at once.
  • Noise and curfew constraints. Residential-adjacent venues frequently cut amplified music at 10 or 11 p.m. Extending means either overtime or an after-party at a bar — budget $1,500–$4,000 if a late night matters to you.

How to Marry in New York City for $30,000–$40,000

Ranked by dollars saved per unit of sacrifice:

  1. Leave Manhattan — saves $14,000–$26,000. Queens, the Bronx, and Staten Island deliver the same vendor pool at dramatically lower venue minimums. This is the biggest lever, and guests will travel.
  2. Skip Saturday — saves 20–35% of venue cost, or $5,000–$10,000. Friday evening and Sunday afternoon carry lower minimums at nearly every NYC venue. A Sunday of a three-day holiday weekend is the single best-value date in the city.
  3. Marry January through March — saves $6,000–$12,000. New York's off-season is genuinely slow, and unlike outdoor-dependent markets, an indoor NYC winter wedding loses nothing to weather. Full seasonal pricing patterns are in our guide to the cheapest month to get married.
  4. Cut the guest list — saves $300 per name. Twenty-five fewer guests is $7,500, and it's the only lever that improves every category at once.
  5. Choose a restaurant buyout over a venue-plus-caterer build — saves $8,000–$15,000 at 50–80 guests. NYC's structural advantage: a great restaurant already owns the kitchen, staff, tables, glassware, and bathrooms you'd otherwise rent. All-in buyouts run $12,000–$30,000 and eliminate half your vendor list. Compare formats side by side with the wedding venue cost calculator.
  6. Do a brunch or lunch reception — saves $3,000–$7,000. Daytime food and bar costs run 25–40% below dinner service at the same venue.

Stack the first four and a $63,000 New York City wedding becomes a $34,000 one with no visible downgrade in quality. Once you've set a target, back into the monthly number with the wedding savings calculator so your deposit schedule and your savings curve don't collide in month seven.

One more NYC-native option worth naming: the City Clerk ceremony plus a real party. A $35 license, a $25 civil ceremony, and a $10,000–$18,000 restaurant dinner for 60 people is a genuinely lovely New York wedding — and it's roughly a quarter of the citywide average.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the average wedding cost in New York City in 2026?

About $63,000 across the five boroughs — roughly 84% above the $34,200 national average. Most NYC weddings fall between $45,000 and $95,000, and the figure assumes 120–125 guests.

Which borough is cheapest to get married in?

Staten Island (about $40,000) and the Bronx (about $42,000), followed by Queens at about $48,000. Manhattan is the most expensive at about $74,000. Moving a 125-guest wedding from Manhattan to Queens saves roughly $26,000.

How much does a wedding venue cost in New York City?

Rental plus food and beverage minimum runs $22,000–$32,000 for a mid-market wedding, about 43% of the budget. Manhattan Saturday minimums commonly sit at $20,000–$45,000. Raw lofts rent for $8,000–$20,000, but you supply catering, rentals, and staff.

How much is a marriage license in New York City?

$35 through the City Clerk, applied for online and collected at an in-person appointment. There's a mandatory 24-hour waiting period before the ceremony, and the license is valid 60 days. A civil ceremony at the Marriage Bureau costs $25 more.

Why are NYC weddings so expensive?

Commercial real estate costs passed through as food and beverage minimums, the highest vendor labor rates in the country, building-related fees (loading dock, freight elevator, COI, security), and 8.875% sales tax stacked on top of a 22–24% service charge.

Can you have a wedding in NYC for under $20,000?

Yes — just not as a 125-guest Saturday reception. A weeknight restaurant buyout for 50–70 guests, a City Clerk ceremony followed by a dinner party, or an outer-borough brunch reception all land between $12,000 and $20,000 all in.

How many guests does the average NYC wedding have?

About 120–125. The budget math is roughly $26,000 in fixed costs plus $300 per guest, which puts 50 guests near $41,000 and 200 guests near $86,000.
