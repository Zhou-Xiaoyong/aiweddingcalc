Ten of your guests will get on a plane. Each one spends $1,376 to watch you get married on Saturday, November 28, 2026, and $412 of that is a ticket priced by the calendar rather than the distance.

Put the other 78 in the same ledger and the room spends $38,332 of its own money attending a wedding that costs you $34,200. That is $4,132 more than your entire budget, paid by people who are not on your spreadsheet and never will be. It is also the real reason 32 names on a 120-person list send a card instead of a reply card.

The Guest's Ledger

One person, flying 900 miles, sharing a hotel room with a partner, arriving Wednesday and leaving Sunday. This is the itinerary a Saturday Thanksgiving weekend date forces, and it is the most expensive shape of a wedding weekend in the US calendar.

Guest expense Basis Amount
Round-trip airfareWed, Nov 25 out, Sun, Nov 29 home$412
Hotel, 3 nights$229 rate + 14.5% tax + $25 nightly fee, half a room$431
Rental car, 4 days$296 all in, split two ways$148
Meals off-siteWed dinner, Thu lunch, Sat breakfast and two coffees$95
OutfitOne dress or suit rental they would not have bought$140
GiftStandard $150 for a close friend$150
Total$1,376

Now spread that across the people who actually say yes. On a 120-name list split 66 local, 34 driving and 20 flying, a Thanksgiving Saturday seats about 88 of them: 56 local, 22 driving, 10 flying.

  • 56 local guests at $212 each (a $62 rideshare round trip and a $150 gift) = $11,872
  • 22 driving guests, 14 of whom take a room at $287 for two nights = $12,700
  • 10 flying guests at $1,376 each = $13,760

Total: $38,332. Your ten flyers spend more between them than your five-piece band costs, and you have never once seen that number on a proposal.

Airfare Is Priced by the Calendar, Not the Route

The Wednesday before Thanksgiving and the Sunday after are the two most marked-up domestic travel days of the year. Your wedding sits at the end of both. A route that prices $412 Wednesday to Sunday prices about $270 Tuesday out and Saturday back, on the same airline, in the same seat.

That $142 gap is $1,420 across your ten flyers. It is also four times what a decent welcome bag costs you per guest, which is worth remembering the next time someone suggests spending the shuttle budget on favors instead.

You cannot move Thanksgiving. You can stop adding to the bill in three ways. Send save-the-dates eight to ten months out rather than six, because holiday inventory on that Wednesday flight clears early and the cheap seats are gone before most couples have picked a venue. Name the airport code, not the city, since a second airport 40 minutes away often prices $80 lower. And put the hotel block link in the save-the-date rather than the invitation, so people are not booking a $229 room twelve weeks out because they waited for a formal card.

Three Nights, No Group Rate

A $229 room bills $287.20 a night once the 14.5% tax and the $25 nightly amenity fee land on it. Three nights is $861.60 per room. Multiply by 30 rooms and your guests are spending $25,848 on beds, which is more than the reception.

Here is the part couples get wrong. Hotels do not need to discount a holiday weekend that sells itself, so the group rate you negotiate often lands within $10 of the public rate or above it, locked ten months before anyone can comparison shop. What the block actually buys you is held inventory, and what it can cost you is attrition.

A standard block with an 80% pickup requirement on 20 rooms means you owe for 16 whether or not they book. If 11 rooms get used, that is five rooms at one night each: $1,435 charged to you, on a card, after the wedding. Ask for a courtesy block instead, by that name. Rooms sit at the public rate, release 30 days out, and carry no penalty. Properties that will not offer one are telling you they do not need your group, which is useful information.

The 260-Mile Guests Decide at 10 P.M.

The guests within a two-hour drive are the swing vote in your final count, and their decision comes down to a single question asked in a hotel lobby at 10 p.m.: drive home tired, or pay $287 for a fourth night.

  • Fuel: 520 miles round trip at 26 mpg and $3.10 a gallon is $62
  • Two nights in the block: $574, if they are not driving home after the last dance
  • Gift: $150, and often less, because the people who traveled already spent $700

So a driving guest costs themselves $786 with a room and $212 without. The $574 swing is larger than anything you can do for them except end the reception earlier, which is why the last section of this article matters more than the first.

There is one line that almost never makes it into a couple's planning and decides more declines than airfare does. Boarding. A kennel runs $45 to $70 a night, holiday weeks sit at the top of that range, and good ones book out in early November. Four nights is $208, landing in the same week as the flight, and the dog-owning couples on your list are usually the first to send regrets. Mention a local sitter in the welcome note and you will move two or three replies.

Five Lines You Actually Control

None of these change what the wedding costs you by much. All of them change what it costs your guests, which changes how many of them come.

Two of these are free and one is the cheapest per-head item on the list. The welcome bag is the only line where more money buys nothing: a $35 tote with a custom tumbler and a koozie costs $3,080 for 88 guests and gets left in the room. Water, a snack, a printed schedule and a phone number for the shuttle cost $12 and get used.

The Shuttle Math

Charter coaches price on a four or five hour minimum plus hourly overage, and the minimum is what traps couples. A 56-passenger coach with a five-hour minimum runs about $1,650 in most markets, with overage around $115 an hour.

Eighty-eight guests at a venue 25 minutes from the block needs two full loops: hotel to venue, back, and back again. That is six hours, not five. Total $1,765, or $20.06 a head.

Rideshare at $38 each way is $76 a head, or $6,688 across the room. The shuttle beats rideshare for the group once about 24 people are riding it, which on an 88-guest wedding is not a close call. What you are really doing is moving $1,765 off 88 people's credit cards and onto yours.

Where it stops making sense: a venue with free parking, a guest list that is 80% local, or a block that is a five-minute walk from the door. In those cases put the $1,765 into the fourth-night subsidy instead, since a discounted Sunday room at $180 does more for a tired driver than a bus they did not need.

The Plus-One Arithmetic

A seat you pay for and nobody fills costs $125.75. A plus-one you invite for a flying guest spends about $795 of their own money to attend, comes out of the same household gift, and sits at table nine next to nobody they know.

Run both numbers and the rule writes itself. Give a plus-one to anyone traveling more than 300 miles, and to anyone who will not recognize a single name on the seating chart. Skip it for the local friend whose college roommates fill a table. That is not a snub; it is the difference between a guest who has a good night and a guest who leaves at 9:30.

The seats that stay empty are the other half of the math. Every unfilled plus-one on your final count is $125.75 you have already committed, which is why the plus-one calculator and the RSVP calculator belong in the same sitting as the invitation list.
