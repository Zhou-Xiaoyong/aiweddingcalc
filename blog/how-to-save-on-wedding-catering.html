How to Save Money on Wedding Catering (17 Proven Hacks)

Catering is usually the single largest line item in a wedding budget — and the one with the most room to cut. The average US couple spends $8,000 – $14,000 just on food and beverage for 150 guests, and small decisions on menu style, timing, and guest count can swing that number by thousands.

The good news: you do not have to serve a sad pasta buffet to save money. Most couples can shave 25-40% off their catering bill with smart choices that guests will never notice — and sometimes even prefer. Below are 17 proven hacks with real 2026 US pricing, a buffet vs plated vs food station comparison, and regional cost data so you know exactly what you should be paying.

How Much Does Wedding Catering Cost in 2026?

In 2026, wedding catering in the US averages $50 – $145 per person for food alone, with the all-in figure (food, beverage, service charge, and tax) landing around $85 – $180 per guest. For a 150-person wedding, that works out to roughly $12,750 – $27,000 total for catering and bar.

Your final number depends on four big levers:

  • Service style: Buffet, plated, family-style, food stations, or food truck
  • Menu tier: Chicken vs steak vs lobster, seasonal vs out-of-season
  • Bar package: Full open bar vs beer & wine vs consumption bar
  • Region: A plated dinner in NYC can cost double the same menu in Kansas City

Service charge (18-22%) and sales tax (6-10% depending on state) are typically added on top of the per-person price, so always ask for the all-in number when comparing quotes.

Buffet vs Plated vs Food Stations: Cost Comparison

Service style is the single biggest catering cost lever. Here's how the main options compare on price, labor, and guest experience for 150 guests in 2026:

Service Style Per Person 150 Guests (Food) Best For
Plated Dinner $65 – $145 $9,750 – $21,750 Formal, elegant weddings
Buffet $50 – $75 $7,500 – $11,250 Casual, variety, big menus
Food Stations $60 – $120 $9,000 – $18,000 Interactive, mingling vibe
Family-Style $55 – $85 $8,250 – $12,750 Cozy, communal feel
Food Truck $13 – $25 $1,950 – $3,750 Casual, outdoor, budget

Notice that buffet runs about $15-70 less per person than plated — for a 150-guest wedding, that's $2,250 – $10,500 in savings just by changing how the food gets to the plate, not what's on it.

Catering Costs by US Region

Where you get married moves the per-person price dramatically. Here are real 2026 ranges for a standard plated dinner (chicken or fish entree, salad, two sides) per person, food only:

  • New York City / Tri-State: $95 – $145 per person
  • San Francisco Bay Area: $90 – $140 per person
  • Los Angeles / Southern California: $80 – $130 per person
  • Chicago / Boston / DC: $75 – $120 per person
  • Seattle / Portland / Denver: $70 – $110 per person
  • Austin / Dallas / Atlanta: $60 – $95 per person
  • Nashville / Charlotte / Raleigh: $55 – $90 per person
  • Midwest (Ohio, Indiana, Iowa): $50 – $80 per person
  • Southeast / Mountain West: $50 – $85 per person
  • Smaller towns / rural: $40 – $65 per person

Buffet pricing in every region runs roughly 25-40% below plated. If you're flexible on location or willing to drive 60-90 minutes outside a major metro, you can often cut per-person catering by 30-50%.

17 Proven Hacks to Save on Wedding Catering

1. Choose a Buffet Over a Plated Dinner

A plated dinner runs $65-145 per person because it requires more kitchen labor, individual plating, and a larger serving staff. A buffet with the same proteins and sides runs $50-75 per person — and many guests actually prefer it because they can choose their portions and skip items they don't want. For 150 guests, this single switch saves $2,250 – $10,500.

2. Book a Friday or Sunday Wedding

Saturdays are peak demand, and caterers (and venues) charge accordingly. Friday and Sunday weddings typically come with 10-25% discounts on both per-person pricing and venue food-and-beverage minimums. Sunday holidays like Labor Day or Sunday of a long weekend can feel like a Saturday to guests but cost like a Sunday.

3. Do a Brunch or Lunch Wedding

Daytime catering is 30-40% cheaper than dinner. Brunch menus use less expensive proteins (eggs, waffles, pastries, brunch cocktails) and lunch portions are smaller. A brunch wedding for 150 guests can run $35-55 per person including a mimosa bar — versus $85-130 for a plated dinner. Bonus: guests drink less alcohol at daytime weddings, so your bar bill drops too.

4. Limit the Guest Count

This is the most powerful lever of all. Catering is priced per person, so every cut flows straight to the bottom line. Cutting from 150 to 120 guests at $85 each saves $2,550 on food alone — plus another $1,500-3,000 on bar, cake, rentals, and favors for those 30 people. Build your list in tiers (A-list, B-list) so you can invite more if budget allows.

5. Skip the Cocktail Hour Appetizers (or Do Passed Hors d'oeuvres Only)

A full stationed appetizer spread during cocktail hour runs $18-35 per person on top of dinner. Two smart alternatives:

  • Skip entirely if dinner is served shortly after the ceremony — saves $2,700-5,250 for 150 guests
  • Passed hors d'oeuvres only at $12-20 per person feels more elegant, controls portions, and saves $6-15 per guest versus stationed

6. Choose a Beer and Wine Only Bar

A full open bar runs $45-85+ per person. A beer and wine bar runs $20-35 per person — that's a 40-60% savings and covers what 80-90% of guests actually drink. Add a signature cocktail or two for a flat fee ($300-600) to make it feel special without opening the liquor cabinet. Consumption-based bars (pay per drink) can also beat flat per-person pricing if your crowd is light on alcohol.

7. Use a Food Truck or Casual Caterer

Food trucks typically charge $13-25 per person — often less than half of traditional catering. They're perfect for casual, outdoor, or non-traditional venues, and they double as entertainment. Many trucks can handle 75-150 guests in 2-3 hours with a pre-set menu. Just confirm they have the permits, insurance, and serving staff your venue requires.

8. Do Family-Style Serving

Family-style service (large platters brought to each table) sits between buffet and plated at $55-85 per person. It cuts plating labor compared to a formal plated dinner, gives guests the variety of a buffet without standing in line, and feels warm and communal. It's a great middle-ground if your venue requires sit-down service but you want to trim cost.

9. Skip the Dessert Course (Use the Wedding Cake Instead)

A separate plated dessert course (after dinner, before cake) adds $8-15 per person. If you're already serving wedding cake — which you are — the extra course is redundant. Cut it and save $1,200-2,250 for 150 guests. If you want variety, add a small DIY dessert bar at $4-8 per person instead.

10. Choose Seasonal and Local Ingredients

Caterers buy at market price, and seasonal produce is cheaper at peak supply. Ask your caterer for a "seasonal menu" using what's abundant in your wedding month — asparagus in spring, tomatoes and corn in summer, squash in fall, root vegetables in winter. Seasonal menus typically run 10-20% cheaper than menus forcing out-of-season ingredients, and they taste better too.

11. Rent a Venue with an In-House Caterer

Venues with in-house catering avoid several hidden fees that external caterers pass on: cake-cutting fees ($2-5/person), corkage fees ($10-25/bottle), outside caterer fees ($500-2,500), rental delivery charges, and double service charges. In-house venues also have set menus at predictable per-person pricing. Compare the all-in cost — many couples find in-house is cheaper once all the add-on fees are counted.

12. DIY the Appetizers or Dessert Bar

If your venue allows outside food (many do for appetizers and dessert, even when they require in-house dinner), you can build a DIY appetizer or dessert bar from Costco, Sam's Club, or a local bakery for $4-8 per person — versus $15-30 catered. Popular DIY options: charcuterie boards, cookie bars, donut walls, popcorn stations, or a candy buffet. Just recruit a friend or day-of coordinator to set it up.

13. Limit Plus-Ones

Every plus-one is a full per-person catering charge. Restricting plus-ones to married, engaged, cohabiting, or long-term-relationship guests typically cuts 15-30 guests from a 150-person list. At $85 per person, that's $1,275-2,550 saved on food alone. Be clear and consistent on invitations to avoid awkward conversations.

14. Choose a Smaller Display Cake + Sheet Cake for Serving

A 150-serving wedding cake from a custom bakery runs $700-1,800. A small 3-tier display cake for photos ($250-450) plus a kitchen sheet cake of the same flavor ($150-300) feeds the same number of guests for 40-60% less. Guests never see the difference — the display cake is cut for photos, and the sheet cake is sliced and plated in the back. This is one of the easiest wins on the list.

15. Negotiate the Service Charge

The service charge (18-22% of food and beverage) is presented as fixed, but it's often negotiable — especially for off-peak dates, large guest counts, or venues that want to hit a quarterly minimum. Tactics that work:

  • Ask if the service charge can be capped at 18% instead of 22%
  • Offer to book a Friday/Sunday in exchange for a reduced service charge
  • Request that the service charge be applied to food only, not bar (saves 18-22% of bar cost)
  • Ask if a higher guaranteed guest count unlocks a lower service percentage

Even a 4-point reduction (from 22% to 18%) on a $12,000 food bill saves $480.

16. Use Grocery Store Flowers for Centerpieces

This one frees up catering budget indirectly. Trader Joe's, Costco, and Sam's Club sell bulk wedding flowers (roses, carnations, eucalyptus, baby's breath) at a fraction of florist pricing — a Costco wedding flower package for 150 guests runs $300-700 versus $2,000-5,000 from a florist. The $1,500-4,000 you save on flowers can be redirected to catering (better menu, open bar, or a buffer for overage).

17. Ask About Off-Season Discounts (January – March)

In most US regions (except Florida, Hawaii, and ski destinations), January through March is off-season. Caterers and venues have empty calendars and will often discount 15-30% to fill dates. Even if the per-person price is firm, you can negotiate on minimums, complimentary upgrades (champagne toast, late-night snack), waived room fees, or extra service hours. The same wedding that costs $35,000 in June can run $24,000-28,000 in February.

Real Savings Example: 150-Guest Wedding

Let's stack these hacks on a real 150-guest wedding to see what's possible. Starting baseline: Saturday plated dinner, full open bar, 150 guests, mid-cost area.

Scenario Per Person 150 Guests Total Savings
Baseline: Sat plated + open bar $130 $19,500 —
Switch to buffet $100 $15,000 –$4,500
Move to Friday (–15%) $85 $12,750 –$2,250
Beer & wine bar (–50% on bar) $70 $10,500 –$2,250
Cut to 130 guests (–20) $70 $9,100 –$1,400
Stacked total $70 $9,100 –$10,400 (53%)

That's over $10,000 in savings from just four moves — buffet, Friday, beer & wine bar, and trimming 20 guests — with no compromise on food quality, just service style, timing, and headcount. Add in a display cake + sheet cake (–$600), grocery store flowers (–$2,000 redirected to catering buffer), and a negotiated service charge (–$500), and you're well past $13,000 saved.
