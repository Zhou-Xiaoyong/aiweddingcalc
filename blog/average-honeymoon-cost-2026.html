Average Honeymoon Cost in 2026: What Couples Really Spend

The trip after the wedding is a real line item — here's the 2026 price range, what moves it, and how to fund the getaway without blowing the budget.

The wedding ends, the dress is packed away, and then there's the trip everyone forgets to budget: the honeymoon. The average honeymoon cost in 2026 is about $4,500–$5,500 for US couples, with luxury or long-haul trips easily passing $10,000. Set against the average US wedding of roughly $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study), the honeymoon is a meaningful add-on — and one couples routinely under-plan. This guide breaks down the real numbers, what moves them, and how to take the trip you want without draining the wedding fund.

The Average Honeymoon Cost in 2026

Here's where couples actually land this year:

  • Short domestic trip (3–5 days): $1,200–$2,500.
  • Standard week (Mexico, Caribbean, US resort): $3,500–$5,500.
  • Mid-range international (Hawaii, Europe): $5,500–$9,000.
  • Luxury / long-haul (Maldives, safari, multi-country): $10,000–$20,000+.

Most US couples cluster around the $4,500–$5,500 mark — a week at an all-inclusive or a relaxed international stay.

What Drives the Cost

Five variables explain most of the spread:

  • Destination. Mexico and the Caribbean beat Hawaii and Europe on bundled value; long-haul Asia or the Maldives costs the most.
  • Duration. Seven to ten nights is the sweet spot; each extra night adds $250–$600 all-in.
  • Season. Shoulder season (late spring, early fall) can be 20–40% cheaper than winter-break or summer peaks.
  • Resort tier. Five-star and over-water villas multiply the room rate; four-star all-inclusives deliver most of the experience for less.
  • Booked separately vs. packaged. Bundled air-hotel-transfers often beats DIY on popular routes.

Cost by Destination

Typical 7-night couple totals in 2026:

  • Mexico (Cancún, Riviera Maya): $3,000–$5,000 all-inclusive — best value per dollar.
  • Caribbean (Jamaica, DR, St. Lucia): $3,500–$6,000 all-inclusive.
  • Hawaii: $5,500–$8,500 (higher airfare from the mainland, no passport needed).
  • Europe (Italy, Greece, France): $6,000–$9,000 with flights and hotels.
  • Domestic US (beach, mountain, city): $1,500–$3,500 by car or short flight.

The Full Cost Breakdown

For a $5,000 average trip, the pieces usually look like:

  • Flights: $600–$1,400 for two.
  • Lodging: $2,000–$3,000 (all-inclusive flips this into one bundled number).
  • Food & drinks: $0 if all-inclusive; $800–$1,500 otherwise.
  • Activities & excursions: $300–$800.
  • Transfers & tips: $150–$400.
  • Travel insurance: $150–$300 (worth it for international trips).

All-inclusive resorts collapse food, drinks, and often activities into the room rate — which is why they read as such good value.

All-Inclusive vs. DIY

  • All-inclusive: one price covers room, meals, drinks, and many activities. Easier to budget, fewer surprise bills, but less local exploration.
  • DIY city or road trip: more flexibility and authenticity, but daily food and transit add up and need closer tracking.
  • Hybrid: all-inclusive resort plus one or two paid excursions gives the best of both.

How Long & When to Go

  • Wait a few days (or weeks) after the wedding? Many couples delay the honeymoon 2–6 weeks to recover and avoid post-wedding exhaustion — and to catch better weather or prices.
  • Shoulder season (April–May, September–early November) saves the most without sacrificing weather in most destinations.
  • Avoid holiday peaks (Presidents' Week, spring break, Christmas) when airfare and resorts spike.

How to Save on the Trip

  • Go all-inclusive to cap daily spend and skip tipping math.
  • Redeem travel points for flights — often the single biggest saving.
  • Book excursions independently rather than through the resort desk.
  • Shoulder-season travel cuts 20–40% on the same room.
  • Ask about honeymoon perks — free nights, upgrades, or a dinner are common with a confirmed wedding date.
  • Trim the room tier one level; spend the difference on experiences.

Honeymoon-Fund Registries

A honeymoon fund is now one of the most popular registry types — guests contribute to the trip instead of (or alongside) physical gifts. It's ideal because:

  • It funds a shared experience rather than more stuff.
  • It lets guests give any amount, from $25 to $500.
  • It keeps the honeymoon off your credit card if you'd otherwise finance it.

Set it up alongside your physical registry using the registry calculator so both friend-tier and family-tier guests find something.

Funding the Trip Inside Your Plan

Decide early whether the honeymoon lives inside the wedding budget or as a separate pot. Either is fine, but plan it in one place so the totals are honest:

  • Inside the wedding budget: treat it as ~13–16% of a $34,200 wedding — about $4,500–$5,500.
  • Separate pot: fund it with a registry and a dedicated savings plan so the wedding-day number stays intact.
  • Model the whole wedding (day + trip) in the wedding budget calculator and the cost estimator so nothing hides.
