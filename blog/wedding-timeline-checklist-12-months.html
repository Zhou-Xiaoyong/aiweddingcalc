You're engaged — congratulations! Now the real work begins. The good news is that with a 12-month engagement, you have plenty of time to plan a beautiful wedding without the panic. The key is knowing what to do and when to do it.

This 12-month wedding planning timeline breaks down every task month by month, so you always know exactly what to focus on. We'll cover when to book wedding vendors, when to send invitations, when to pay deposits, and what to do in those final nerve-wracking weeks. Plus, we've included shortened 6-month and 3-month rush timelines for couples on a tighter schedule.

Why a 12-Month Timeline Matters

A 12-month engagement is the sweet spot for wedding planning. It's long enough that the best venues and vendors are still available, but short enough that you maintain momentum and don't lose steam. Here's why following a structured timeline is so important:

  • Vendors book up fast: The best photographers, DJs, and florists in popular wedding markets are reserved 9–12 months in advance.
  • Dresses take time: Wedding dresses are made to order and can take 6–9 months to arrive, plus 2–3 months for alterations.
  • You avoid rush fees: Last-minute orders for invitations, favors, and attire almost always cost more.
  • Stress stays low: Spreading tasks across 12 months means you never face a mountain of decisions at once.
  • Your budget stretches further: More time means more paychecks to spread deposits and final payments across.

If your engagement is shorter than 12 months, don't panic — just skip to our 6-month rush timeline or 3-month rush timeline sections.

12 Months Out: Lay the Foundation

The first month is all about setting the stage. Every decision you make here cascades into the rest of your timeline, so take these steps seriously.

Your 12-Month Checklist

  • Set your total wedding budget: Sit down with anyone contributing (families, partners) and agree on a hard number. Use our wedding budget calculator to break it into categories.
  • Pick a wedding date (or 2–3 options): Have a primary date plus backups, since venue availability may force a shift.
  • Book your venue: This is the single most time-sensitive decision. Popular venues book 12+ months out, especially for Saturday dates in wedding season.
  • Start a rough guest list: You don't need final names yet, but you need a headcount range — venue capacity and per-person catering minimums depend on it.
  • Hire a wedding planner (optional): Full-service planners typically cost 10–15% of your total budget. If you're DIY-ing, at least consider a day-of coordinator later.
  • Open a wedding bank account: A separate checking or savings account keeps deposits, family contributions, and vendor payments clean and easy to track.

11 Months Out: Lock In Key Vendors

Now that your venue is booked, secure the vendors who book out fastest. Photography and videography are top priority — great ones go quickly.

Your 11-Month Checklist

  • Book your photographer: Tour portfolios, check availability for your date, and sign the contract. Most require a 25–50% deposit.
  • Book your videographer: If you want video, book this now too. Booking photo and video from the same studio can save 10–20%.
  • Book your caterer (if not in-house): If your venue doesn't require their catering, now is the time to lock in a caterer and do a rough menu direction.
  • Start wedding dress shopping: Try on silhouettes at 2–3 boutiques. Dresses take 6–9 months to arrive, so you need to order soon.
  • Research DJs and bands: Watch videos, read reviews, and request quotes from 3–5 options. You'll book them next month.

10 Months Out: Music, Flowers & Officiant

With photo, video, and catering handled, turn to the vendors who shape the atmosphere of your day.

Your 10-Month Checklist

  • Book your DJ or band: See them live if possible. Get every detail — setup time, breaks, overtime rate, MC duties — in the contract.
  • Book your florist: Share your venue photos, color palette, and mood board. Florists book 6–10 months out in busy markets.
  • Send save-the-dates: Guests need 6–12 months of notice for destination weddings, 4–6 months for local ones. Use our save-the-date calculator to time it right.
  • Book your officiant: Religious officiants may require premarital counseling; civil officiants book quickly for popular dates.
  • Start marriage prep: If your faith requires counseling or classes (Catholic Engaged Encounter, etc.), sign up now — these programs have limited spots and span several weeks.

9 Months Out: Transportation, Beauty & Rings

Move on to logistics, beauty, attire for the wedding party, and your rings.

Your 9-Month Checklist

  • Book transportation: Limos, party buses, or shuttles for the wedding party and guests between ceremony, reception, and hotels.
  • Book your hair and makeup artist: Schedule a trial for a few weeks before the wedding. Book the same artist for bridesmaids if you want a unified look.
  • Choose bridesmaid dresses: Pick a color and length, then let each bridesmaid order in the silhouette that flatters her. Order 6+ months ahead for sizing lead time.
  • Book the rehearsal dinner venue: Traditionally hosted by the groom's family, but anyone can host. Book early — popular restaurants fill up fast.
  • Order your wedding rings: Custom rings take 2–4 months. Even off-the-shelf rings may need resizing, so order now to leave a buffer.

8 Months Out: Honeymoon, Menu & Suits

Time to plan the after-party (the honeymoon), finalize the food, and get the groom's attire moving.

Your 8-Month Checklist

  • Book a photo booth: Photo booths are one of the most popular wedding add-ons. Book now to lock in your preferred style and props.
  • Plan your honeymoon: Research destinations, check passport expiration dates, and book flights and resorts. Use our honeymoon budget calculator to set a realistic spend.
  • Finalize the menu and do a tasting: Sit down with your caterer to taste each course. Confirm dietary accommodations for guests.
  • Book a hotel room block: Reserve 10–30 rooms at a nearby hotel at a discounted rate for out-of-town guests.
  • Start suit or tux shopping: Whether buying or renting, start now so there's time for tailoring and fittings.

7 Months Out: Invitations & Ceremony

Shift your attention to the paper goods and the ceremony itself.

Your 7-Month Checklist

  • Order invitations: Allow 6–8 weeks for printing, addressing, and assembly. Order 10–15% extra in case of mistakes or last-minute additions.
  • Choose ceremony readings: Pick 1–3 readings and decide who will read them. Religious ceremonies may have required readings.
  • Plan the ceremony structure: Work with your officiant on the order of events — processional, readings, vows, ring exchange, unity ceremony, recessional.
  • Buy wedding shoes and accessories: Get shoes early so you can break them in. Buy undergarments, jewelry, veil, and any hairpieces.

6 Months Out: Mail Invitations & Cake

Six months out, invitations go in the mail and you lock in the cake and final flowers.

Your 6-Month Checklist

  • Mail invitations: Send 8–10 weeks before the wedding (12 weeks for destination). Set an RSVP deadline 3–4 weeks before the date.
  • Book the cake baker: Schedule a tasting and confirm design, size, and delivery. Our wedding cake calculator helps size the cake to your guest count.
  • Finalize flowers: Confirm bouquets, boutonnieres, centerpieces, ceremony arrangements, and any repurposing plans.
  • Plan transportation logistics: Map out the timeline for getting the wedding party and guests from point A to B to C.
  • Start premarital counseling: If not already underway, begin any required counseling or sessions now so you finish well before the wedding.

5 Months Out: Favors & Coordinator

Details start to come together — favors, rehearsal dinner, and possibly a day-of coordinator.

Your 5-Month Checklist

  • Order favors: Whether edible, functional, or charitable, order early — especially if they're personalized. Use our favor cost calculator to budget.
  • Plan rehearsal dinner details: Finalize the menu, guest list, and any toasts or activities. Send rehearsal dinner invites soon.
  • Finalize bridesmaid attire: Confirm all bridesmaids have ordered their dresses and have a tailoring timeline.
  • Book a day-of coordinator (if not already): Even if you DIY most planning, a day-of coordinator (typically $1,500–$2,500) saves your sanity on the wedding day.

4 Months Out: License & Programs

Handle the legalities and the ceremony paper goods.

Your 4-Month Checklist

  • Apply for your marriage license: Rules vary by state and county. Some licenses are valid immediately, others have a waiting period. Check your local requirements and apply accordingly — don't get it too early, as most expire in 30–90 days.
  • Finalize ceremony details: Confirm readings, music cues, and the officiant's script. Decide on any unity ceremony elements.
  • Plan the reception timeline: Map out cocktail hour, dinner, toasts, first dance, parent dances, cake cutting, dancing, and send-off with exact times.
  • Order wedding programs: If you're having them, order now. Many couples skip programs today, but they're nice for religious or multi-cultural ceremonies.

3 Months Out: Fittings & Seating Chart

The home stretch. Now it's about confirming, refining, and finalizing.

Your 3-Month Checklist

  • Schedule your final dress fitting: This is usually 4–6 weeks before the wedding, but schedule it now. Bring the shoes and undergarments you'll wear on the day.
  • Finalize the guest count: Tally RSVPs, follow up with stragglers, and lock in the final headcount for your caterer and venue.
  • Confirm all vendors: Reconfirm every vendor in writing — date, time, location, services, and final payment due dates.
  • Create the seating chart: Once RSVPs are in, build your seating chart. Our seating chart planner makes this easy.
  • Send rehearsal dinner invites: Send formal invites to everyone in the wedding party, immediate family, and any out-of-town guests you're including.

2 Months Out: Menu, Cake & Vows

Finalize the sensory details — food, cake, vows, and music.

Your 2-Month Checklist

  • Finalize the menu: Give your caterer the final headcount, dietary restrictions, and any last-minute menu adjustments.
  • Confirm the cake design: Lock in size, flavor, design, and delivery logistics with your baker.
  • Write your vows: If you're writing your own vows, start drafting now. Practice reading them aloud to time them.
  • Finalize the music playlist: Send your DJ or band your must-play list, do-not-play list, and songs for special dances.
  • Confirm transportation: Reconfirm pickup times, addresses, and contact info for all drivers.

1 Month Out: License & Walkthrough

One month to go. Everything shifts from planning to confirming.

Your 1-Month Checklist

  • Get your marriage license: Pick it up (or apply, depending on your state) within the validity window. Bring ID and any required documents.
  • Final venue walkthrough: Visit the venue with your coordinator or planner to confirm layout, flow, and decor placement.
  • Confirm final headcount: Give the caterer and venue the final, locked-in guest count. Most need this 1–2 weeks out.
  • Finalize the seating chart: Print place cards, escort cards, or a seating chart display.
  • Pack a day-of emergency kit: Include safety pins, tape, stain remover, pain relievers, mints, bandages, a sewing kit, and phone charger.

2 Weeks Out: Final Confirmations

Two weeks out is the final confirmation sprint. Touch base with every vendor and break in those shoes.

Your 2-Week Checklist

  • Send final vendor confirmations: Email every vendor to reconfirm arrival time, contact info, and final payment status.
  • Finalize the ceremony timeline: Confirm processional order, music cues, and timing with your officiant.
  • Break in your wedding shoes: Wear them around the house for short stretches to avoid blisters on the big day.
  • Confirm your hair and makeup trial: Schedule the trial for 2–4 weeks before the wedding so the look is fresh in your artist's mind.

1 Week Out: Delegate & Relax

The week of the wedding, your only job is to enjoy it. Hand off the logistics.

Your 1-Week Checklist

  • Delegate day-of tasks: Assign someone to handle vendor tips, transport the rings, wrangle the wedding party, and answer questions.
  • Pack for the honeymoon: Get your luggage ready, confirm travel documents, and arrange airport transportation.
  • Confirm rehearsal dinner details: Finalize the time, location, guest list, and any toasts with the host.
  • Relax!: Get a massage, take a bath, see a movie — do whatever helps you unwind. You've planned a beautiful wedding. Now enjoy it.

Day Before: Rehearsal & Rest

The day before is the calm before the celebration. Run through the ceremony, then rest.

Day-Before Checklist

  • Attend the ceremony rehearsal: Walk through the processional, recessional, and any ceremony logistics with the wedding party and officiant.
  • Rehearsal dinner: Enjoy this intimate gathering — it's often the only time both families have together before the wedding.
  • Drop off decor at the venue: Bring any centerpieces, signage, favors, or personal items to the venue so you're not rushing in the morning.
  • Get some sleep: Aim for 8 hours. Drink water, skip the alcohol, and lay out everything you need for the morning.

Shorter Timeline: 6-Month Rush Plan

Not every couple has 12 months. If you're planning a wedding in 6 months, you can absolutely do it — you just need to compress the timeline and act fast. Here's how to condense the 12-month plan into 6 months:

Months 1–2 (Combine 12 + 11 + 10 + 9 + 8 months)

  • Set budget, pick date, book venue — all in week 1.
  • Book photographer, videographer, caterer, DJ/band, florist, and officiant as fast as possible (within 4–6 weeks).
  • Start dress shopping immediately — you may need to buy off-the-rack or pay a rush fee.
  • Book transportation, hair and makeup, rehearsal dinner venue, and photo booth.
  • Order wedding rings and start suit shopping.
  • Send save-the-dates right away.

Months 3–4 (Combine 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 months)

  • Order invitations (expedite printing if needed) and mail them 10 weeks out.
  • Book cake baker and finalize menu with a tasting.
  • Plan honeymoon and book hotel room block.
  • Order favors, programs, and any paper goods.
  • Apply for marriage license and plan ceremony structure.
  • Book a day-of coordinator — you'll need one more than ever.

Months 5–6 (Combine 3 + 2 + 1 months)

  • Final dress fitting, finalize guest count, confirm all vendors.
  • Build seating chart, finalize menu and cake, write vows.
  • Final venue walkthrough, pack day-of emergency kit.
  • Two weeks out: final vendor confirmations, break in shoes, hair and makeup trial.
  • One week out: delegate, pack for honeymoon, relax.

Biggest risk with a 6-month timeline: Top-tier vendors and Saturday venue dates may already be booked. Be flexible on the date (Friday or Sunday opens up more options) and be ready to book the moment you find something you love.

Shorter Timeline: 3-Month Rush Plan

A 3-month engagement is intense but doable, especially for smaller weddings. You'll need to be decisive and willing to compromise on a few things.

Month 1

  • Set budget, pick date, and book venue — same week.
  • Book photographer, caterer, officiant, and DJ/band immediately. Expect to take whoever's available.
  • Buy your wedding dress off-the-rack (sample sale or ready-to-ship). Schedule rush alterations.
  • Book hair and makeup, florist, and transportation.
  • Send save-the-dates by text or email — no time for paper.
  • Apply for the marriage license (check your state's waiting period).

Month 2

  • Mail invitations with a short RSVP window (3 weeks).
  • Order wedding rings (off-the-shelf, not custom).
  • Book cake baker and schedule a tasting.
  • Plan honeymoon and book hotel room block.
  • Order favors and any paper goods with expedited shipping.
  • Book rehearsal dinner venue.

Month 3

  • Final dress fitting, finalize guest count, confirm all vendors.
  • Build seating chart, finalize menu and cake, write vows.
  • Final venue walkthrough, pack day-of emergency kit.
  • Hire a day-of coordinator — non-negotiable for a 3-month timeline.
  • One week out: delegate, pack for honeymoon, relax.

Biggest risk with a 3-month timeline: Custom anything is off the table. You'll buy off-the-rack, choose from available (not dream) vendors, and likely have a smaller guest list. Keep the wedding small and simple — a courthouse or backyard wedding with 30–50 guests is very doable in 3 months.

Budget Milestones: When to Pay

Knowing when to pay deposits and final balances is just as important as knowing when to book. Here's a typical payment schedule for a 12-month engagement:

Deposits (Due at Booking)

Most vendors require a deposit to hold your date. Typical deposit amounts:

  • Venue: 25–50% of total (due at booking, 12 months out)
  • Photographer: 25–50% (due at booking, 11 months out)
  • Videographer: 25–50% (due at booking, 11 months out)
  • Caterer: 25–50% (due at booking, 11 months out)
  • DJ/band: 20–30% (due at booking, 10 months out)
  • Florist: 25% (due at booking, 10 months out)
  • Officiant: 50–100% (due at booking, 10 months out)
  • Transportation: 25–50% (due at booking, 9 months out)
  • Hair and makeup: 25–50% (due at booking, 9 months out)
  • Cake baker: 25–50% (due at booking, 6 months out)
  • Day-of coordinator: 25–50% (due at booking, 5+ months out)

Final Payments (Due 1–2 Weeks Before Wedding)

Most vendors collect the final balance 1–2 weeks before the wedding. Some venues require it the day of. Plan to have all final payments ready by the 2-week mark:

  • Venue: Final balance due 2 weeks before (sometimes day-of)
  • Caterer: Final balance due 1–2 weeks before, based on final headcount
  • Photographer: Final balance due 1–2 weeks before
  • Videographer: Final balance due 1–2 weeks before
  • DJ/band: Final balance due 1–2 weeks before
  • Florist: Final balance due 1 week before
  • Transportation: Final balance due 1 week before
  • Hair and makeup: Final balance due day-of or 1 week before
  • Cake baker: Final balance due 1–2 weeks before
  • Day-of coordinator: Final balance due 1 week before

Gratuities (Day-Of)

Don't forget tips. Plan 15–20% of catering cost, $50–$100 for the photographer, $50–$150 for the DJ/band, $20–$50 each for hair and makeup artists, and $20–$50 for the driver. Pre-count these into labeled envelopes the week of the wedding and hand them to your day-of coordinator.

Common Mistakes by Timeline

Every timeline has its traps. Here are the most common mistakes couples make — and how to avoid them.

Common 12-Month Mistakes

  • Booking the venue before setting a budget: You fall in love with a venue you can't afford, then blow the entire budget.
  • Waiting too long to order the dress: Dresses take 6–9 months. Order by month 2 or you'll pay rush fees.
  • Forgetting to start marriage prep early: Required counseling programs span weeks. Sign up by month 10.
  • Skipping the day-of coordinator: Even with 12 months of planning, you need someone running the show on the day.

Common 6-Month Mistakes

  • Trying to do everything yourself: A 6-month timeline is too compressed for full DIY. Hire help.
  • Insisting on a Saturday: Saturdays book first. A Friday or Sunday opens up better venues and vendors.
  • Ordering custom invitations: Custom printing takes 8–10 weeks. Use semi-custom or digital printing.
  • Forgetting the hotel room block: Hotels fill up fast in season. Book the room block in month 1.

Common 3-Month Mistakes

  • Trying to invite 200 people: A 3-month timeline works best with 50 guests or fewer. Keep it intimate.
  • Ordering a custom dress: There's no time. Buy off-the-rack or sample.
  • Skipping the day-of coordinator: At 3 months, a coordinator isn't optional — it's the only way you'll enjoy your day.
  • Forgetting the marriage license waiting period: Some states have a 3–5 day waiting period. Apply early in month 3.
