A wedding under $15,000 with 90 guests works out to $167 a person in 2026. The breakdown that gets you there is not a list of things you skipped. It is a list of people you hired, plus a fixed block of costs that eats half the money before a single plate leaves the kitchen.

Fifteen thousand dollars is enough for a catered dinner with staff, a DJ who can run a room, six hours of photography, and 90 guests. It is not enough for a florist, a videographer, and a plated service at the same time.

The $7,500 Fixed Floor Nobody Quotes You

Most wedding budgets are built from the top down: here is the total, here is what percentage goes to flowers. That method fails at $15,000, because six categories have a floor you cannot negotiate below without losing the service entirely.

Category Practical Floor What the Floor Actually Buys
Venue$800Park pavilion, community hall, or lodge on a Sunday, tables and chairs included, your people set up
Photography$1,500Four hours, one shooter, edited digital files, no album and no second photographer
Catering, drop-off$35/headFood in pans, no staff, you rent chafing dishes and hire two people to serve
Catering, staffed buffet$52/headServers at one per 10 guests, clearing, cake cutting, coffee service, equipment included
DJ$600Four hours, own sound and lights, no uplighting and no ceremony audio
Officiant$150A 30-minute ceremony, no rehearsal attendance, no custom vows consultation
Flowers$400Bulk greenery, candles, and a bouquet, arranged by you or a friend the morning of

Add those floors to attire, cake, hair, and the venue's rentals and you land near $7,500. That figure does not care how many people you invite. It is the same $7,500 at 60 guests and at 150.

This is what breaks most $15,000 plans. Couples cut the guest list first, assuming headcount is the big lever. It is second. The fixed block is first, and it is the only place where one decision moves four figures. A $1,600 hall instead of a $6,000 estate is not a taste compromise. It is 56 guests' worth of food and drink.

Three rows carry all the risk.

Catering at $52 a head sits in the middle of the 2026 buffet range of $50 to $70 and about half the $95 national average for plated. Ninety people turns that into $4,680. Drop to $35 for pan delivery and you save $1,530, but you lose the servers, the chafing dishes, the clearing, and the cake cutting. That money is labor, and guests can tell by 8 p.m. Model the menu in the wedding catering calculator before you sign.

The $1,240 service and tax line is a 20% service charge on the $5,760 food-and-beverage subtotal, plus about $90 of tax on the charge itself. In states that tax the full food-and-beverage total, this row runs $300 to $400 higher, and plenty of venues charge 22% to 24% rather than 20%. Four points on $5,760 is $230 that never appears on a menu. Run your contract through the tax and tip calculator to see the stack your state produces.

Rentals at $450 only holds if the venue owns tables and chairs. Linens run $18 to $30 each for nine tables, napkins $0.75 to $1.50 apiece, glassware $3 to $5 a setting. A venue that advertises "tables included" and nothing else turns this row into $700 to $900. Ask which of the four it owns before you compare two quotes.

Read the last row carefully. At 150 guests there is $34 a head after the service charge, and the cheapest pan-delivery BBQ in most metros starts at $32 and arrives cold. That is not a reception. That is a picnic with a DJ.

The One Splurge Rule, Priced in Guests

Every budget guide tells you to pick one thing to splurge on. None of them tell you what it costs. At 90 guests, the marginal cost of one more guest, including food, drink, and service, is about $78. Divide any upgrade by $78 and you get its price in names.

Upgrade at 90 Guests Added Cost Cost in Guests
Plated dinner at $95 instead of buffet at $52$4,64460 of your 90 guests
Estate venue instead of a flat-fee hall$4,40056 guests
Live band instead of a DJ$2,38030 guests
Premium open bar instead of beer and wine$2,30430 guests
Videographer$2,20028 guests
Florist-designed centerpieces instead of greenery$1,25016 guests

A plated dinner costs 60 of your 90 guests. That is not a rhetorical flourish; it is $4,644 divided by $78. Most couples run that math, look at their list, and keep the buffet. The plated upgrade earns its keep when the list is 40 people and the room is a restaurant, which is a different budget entirely.

The videographer is the cheapest real upgrade at 28 guests and the one most couples regret skipping. If two of your 100 names are grandparents in their eighties, spend the $2,200 and cut the list to 62. Nobody at a 62-guest wedding complains about the room. Plenty wish they had video of a parent who has since passed.

Three Lines Couples Get Wrong at This Tier

They treat the service charge as a tip. A 20% service charge on catering is not automatically gratuity. In many venues it covers staffing logistics, insurance, and equipment, and the servers see none of it. Ask in writing whether it is distributed to staff as gratuity. If the answer is vague, budget $20 to $40 per server in cash. At 90 guests with nine servers that is $180 to $360 you did not plan for.

They forget the guaranteed count. Caterers bill a guarantee you confirm 72 hours out, and the standard instruction is to guarantee 5% to 10% above your RSVP total. Ninety acceptances becomes a guarantee of 95 to 99. At $52 a head that is $260 to $468 for plates nobody eats, unless you ask in advance whether unserved meals can be boxed. Most caterers will box ten.

They miss vendor meals and delivery. Eight vendors on site for six hours eat. Vendor meals run $25 to $45 each, and many contracts put that at 50% of the guest plate, so 8 × $30 is $240 that appears on the final invoice. Delivery and setup fees add $150 to $400 per vendor for florals, cake, and rentals. Add $400 to $650 to any $15,000 plan and it stops being $15,000.

Trim to $12,000 or Stretch to $18,000?

Cutting from 90 guests to 75 saves $1,170, not the $2,500 that per-guest arithmetic suggests, because the fixed block does not shrink when you remove names. You still pay the photographer, the DJ, the dress, and the same nine tables' worth of rentals if the room is booked.

Growing from 90 to 110 adds $1,560 in food and drink plus about $400 for a third bartender, so roughly $1,960. If you are at 97 names and the budget is at $15,000, invite the 97 and hold the buffet. Cutting seven people to save $546 is the worst trade in wedding planning.

Reaching $18,000 takes either 38 more guests at $78 or one upgrade from the table above. Reaching $12,000 means cutting $3,000, and that much money only lives in one place: the venue. Compare the two venue models in the wedding venue cost calculator before you fall in love with a room.

Deposits, Guarantees, and the 30-Day Invoice

Fifteen thousand dollars does not leave your account the way it appears in a spreadsheet. It leaves in six payments, and the last one is the biggest.

  • 12 months out: venue deposit, 25% to 50% of the fee, often non-refundable.
  • 9 to 10 months out: photographer retainer, 25% to 50% of $1,850. The best shooters in the $1,500 to $2,500 band book out 10 months ahead on Saturdays.
  • 6 months out: caterer deposit of $1,170 to $2,340, plus the DJ retainer.
  • 30 days out: final guest count, final menu, and the venue balance. This is the week the invoice grows.
  • 7 days out: $450 to $600 in cash for tips and vendor meals, in labeled envelopes.
  • Day of: the guarantee invoice, if your catering contract bills after service.

On the savings side, $15,000 over 18 months is $833 a month; over 12 it is $1,250. A high-yield account at 4% earns about $300 on an average $7,500 balance across 18 months, which pays the vendor meals. Set the monthly transfer the week you set the date. Ninety guests on $15,000 is a real wedding with staff, hot food, a DJ, and six hours of photographs. It has a $7,500 floor under it that no amount of negotiation removes, and once you can see that floor, the guest list stops being an argument and becomes arithmetic. Book the $1,600 venue, put $4,680 into the buffet, and hold $516 back for the invoice that always shows up in the last week.
