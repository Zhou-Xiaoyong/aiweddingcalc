You just got engaged — and you want to be married in three months. Take a breath: a 3 month wedding planning checklist is not only possible, it's more common than you'd think. Short engagements skip the decision fatigue of an 18-month slog, keep your momentum high, and stop the guest list (and the budget) from quietly ballooning. The trade-off is speed: you have to be decisive, flexible on date, and willing to buy ready-made instead of custom.

This guide is a complete, week-by-week run of exactly what to do in the 90 days before your wedding — what to book first, when to send invitations, how to pay deposits, and the mistakes that sink a short timeline. If you've got more time, our 12-month wedding planning timeline and 6-month wedding planning timeline are worth a skim too — but if the clock is ticking, start here.

Why a 3-Month Timeline Works (and Who It's For)

A 90-day engagement works best for weddings in the 30–75 guest range, and for couples who are comfortable making fast decisions. The national average US wedding costs about $34,200 (The Knot 2026), but shorter engagements almost always end up smaller and simpler — which is the single biggest natural budget-saver there is. You're not cutting corners; you're right-sizing the event to the time you have.

Three things make a 3-month wedding realistic: digital invitations and save-the-dates (no 8-week print window), off-the-rack attire (no 6-month dress order), and a day-of coordinator who absorbs the logistics you don't have time to manage. Get those three right and the rest falls into place.

Month 1 of Your 3-Month Wedding Planning Checklist

Month 1 is pure triage: book the four vendors that disappear first, then lock the legal stuff and the big-ticket personal items.

  • Week 1 — Venue, photographer, caterer, officiant. These four book up fastest. Pick a date with 2–3 backups (Friday and Sunday open far more options than Saturday) and book all four within the first seven days. If your venue has in-house catering, that's one fewer vendor to chase.
  • Week 1 — Send digital save-the-dates. With 12 weeks to go, paper is out. Text, email, or a free wedding website does the job. Give guests a firm headcount ask early.
  • Week 2 — Attire, off-the-rack. Buy a sample or ready-to-ship dress and book rush alterations. Groom and groomsmen can rent suits in days.
  • Week 2 — Hair, makeup, florist, transportation. Book these now; they fill quickly in peak season.
  • Week 3 — Marriage license. Check your state's waiting period and validity window (many expire in 30–90 days), then apply so it's ready well before the day.
  • Week 4 — Photographer/videographer contract review & honeymoon outline. Confirm deliverables and start rough honeymoon plans even if you book flights next month.

By the end of month 1 your venue, food, photos, and officiant should all be signed. That's the hard part done.

Month 2: Invitations, Cake & Details

Now you shift from booking to building the experience. You have about 4–8 weeks until the wedding, so invitations go out now with a short RSVP window (3 weeks).

  • Week 5 — Invitations with a 3-week RSVP deadline. Use digital or semi-custom printing with expedited shipping. Order wedding rings off-the-shelf (not custom).
  • Week 6 — Cake baker + tasting, honeymoon booking, hotel room block. Book the baker and confirm design; lock flights and resort; reserve 10–30 rooms at a group rate for out-of-town guests.
  • Week 7 — Favors, paper goods, rehearsal dinner venue. Order any favors with expedited shipping and book the rehearsal dinner spot.
  • Week 8 — Finalize menu, music playlist, vows draft, day-of coordinator. A month-of coordinator is non-negotiable on a 90-day timeline. Hire one now if you haven't.

Month 3: Confirm, Fit & Relax

The final month is all confirming and refining. You've booked everything; now make sure it actually happens.

  • Week 9 — Final dress fitting, confirmed headcount, vendor reconfirmation. Reconfirm every vendor in writing: date, time, location, services, and final payment due dates.
  • Week 10 — Seating chart, cake design lock, hair/makeup trial. Build the seating chart with our wedding checklist priority calculator to keep tasks in the right order.
  • Week 11 — Final venue walkthrough, day-of emergency kit, marriage license pickup. Walk the layout with your coordinator and pack the kit (safety pins, tape, stain remover, chargers).
  • Week 12 — Delegate, confirm rehearsal dinner, relax. Hand day-of tasks to your wedding party and actually enjoy the final week.

The 90-Day Week-by-Week Sprint (Compact)

If you want the whole thing on one screen, here's the compressed version of the 3 month wedding planning checklist:

  • Days 1–7: Date + backups, venue, photographer, caterer, officiant, digital save-the-dates.
  • Days 8–14: Off-the-rack dress + rush alterations, book hair/makeup, florist, transportation.
  • Days 15–21: Apply for marriage license, review photo/video contracts.
  • Days 22–30: Outline honeymoon, book any must-have rentals.
  • Days 31–45: Invitations (3-week RSVP), off-the-shelf rings.
  • Days 46–60: Cake + tasting, honeymoon flights/hotel, room block, favors, rehearsal dinner venue.
  • Days 61–75: Menu lock, playlist, vows, hire month-of coordinator.
  • Days 76–90: Final fittings, headcount, vendor confirmations, seating chart, walkthrough, delegate, marry.

Set a Realistic 3-Month Budget

The average US wedding runs about $34,200 (The Knot 2026), but a 90-day wedding is usually smaller — and that's the point. A 50-guest intimate wedding can land between $10,000 and $20,000 depending on venue and city, while a 75-guest version sits higher. Don't assume the national average applies; build your own number.

3-Month Mistakes to Avoid

  • Insisting on 150+ guests. A 90-day timeline shines at 30–75 guests. Forcing a big crowd means more catering, more rentals, and a venue that's already booked.
  • Ordering anything custom. Custom dresses, custom invitations, custom rings — none have the lead time. Buy ready-made.
  • Skipping the day-of coordinator. At three months, a coordinator isn't a luxury; it's the only way you'll enjoy your day.
  • Forgetting the marriage license waiting period. Apply early in month 2 so a 3–5 day wait never becomes a crisis.
  • Booking a Saturday out of habit. Fridays and Sundays open far more venues and vendors on short notice — and often cost less.
