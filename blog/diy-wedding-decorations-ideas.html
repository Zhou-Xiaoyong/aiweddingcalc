15 DIY Wedding Decoration Ideas That Look Expensive (On a Budget)

Wedding decorations are one of the easiest categories to overspend on — and one of the easiest to DIY without anyone noticing. The average US couple spends $2,000 – $5,000 on decor and flowers, yet most of the "wow" moments at a wedding (the centerpieces, the welcome sign, the photo backdrop, the ceremony arch) can be built at home for a fraction of the professional price.

The trick is knowing which projects are worth your time and which ones will leave you crying into a hot glue gun at 2 a.m. the night before. Below are 15 DIY wedding decoration ideas that genuinely look expensive, with real 2026 US costs, a materials list, and step-by-step instructions for each — plus a no-nonsense list of what you should never DIY.

Why DIY Your Wedding Decor?

Professional wedding decor carries a massive markup. Florists and rental companies charge for design time, materials, delivery, setup, teardown, and the risk of breakage — and rightly so. But if you're willing to put in the labor yourself (and recruit a few willing friends), you can capture most of that value. A $40 professional centerpiece often contains $4 worth of materials.

DIY decor also gives you total creative control. You can match your exact color palette, repurpose family heirlooms, and build one-of-a-kind pieces no rental company stocks. The catch is time: most of these projects take 1-4 hours each, so start 2-3 months out and knock out one project per weekend.

A quick rule of thumb: DIY projects with a 3x or higher savings ratio are almost always worth it. All 15 ideas below clear that bar — most save 5x to 10x the cost of buying or renting the professional equivalent.

15 DIY Wedding Decoration Ideas

1. Mason Jar Centerpieces with Fairy Lights

DIY cost: $3-5 each  ·  Professional equivalent: $40+ each  ·  Savings: ~$35+ per centerpiece

These are the quintessential budget centerpiece — and for good reason. A cluster of three mason jars in varying heights, filled with warm-white fairy lights and wrapped in twine or ribbon, glows beautifully on a reception table and photographs like a much pricier candle arrangement.

Materials needed:

  • Mason jars (3 per table) — Dollar Tree or thrift stores, ~$1 each
  • Battery-operated fairy lights (warm white) — Amazon 12-pack for $12 (~$1 each)
  • Jute twine or satin ribbon — $4 for a 100ft roll (enough for 20+ jars)
  • Optional: artificial eucalyptus sprigs or baby's breath — $6 for a bundle

Step-by-step:

  1. Wash and fully dry the mason jars, then remove any label residue with rubbing alcohol.
  2. Coil a string of fairy lights loosely and drop it into each jar, tucking the battery pack at the bottom or taping it to the inside of the lid.
  3. Tie twine or ribbon around the rim of each jar in a small bow.
  4. Cluster three jars of varying heights (pint, quart, half-gallon) in the center of the table and tuck a sprig of greenery between them.
  5. Test the lights the night before and stock extra AA batteries for your day-of helper.

2. Pallet Wood Welcome Sign

DIY cost: $15  ·  Professional equivalent: $75+  ·  Savings: ~$60

A hand-lettered welcome sign on a pallet at the ceremony entrance sets the tone immediately and replaces a $75+ rental. The rustic-chic look works for barn, garden, backyard, and vineyard weddings alike.

Materials needed:

  • Wooden pallet (free from local breweries, garden centers, or Facebook Marketplace) or 3-4 fence pickets ($8)
  • Sandpaper (medium + fine grit) — $4
  • White or wood-colored paint pen / chalk marker — $5
  • Two L-brackets or a wooden stake for standing — $3
  • Optional: wood stain or whitewash — $6

Step-by-step:

  1. Source a pallet and pry off any broken boards; cut to a uniform size if needed.
  2. Sand every surface thoroughly to avoid splinters — this is the step you cannot skip.
  3. Apply stain or whitewash and let it dry completely (24 hours).
  4. Print your wording (names, date, a short welcome line) in the font and size you want, then rub pencil graphite on the back of the paper and trace over it to transfer the outline onto the wood.
  5. Fill in the transferred outline with your paint pen, working slowly for crisp lines.
  6. Attach L-brackets to the back so the sign stands upright, or mount it on a stake to push into the ground.

3. Paper Flower Backdrop

DIY cost: $25  ·  Professional equivalent: $200+  ·  Savings: ~$175

A wall of giant paper flowers behind the sweetheart table or photo booth is one of the highest-impact, lowest-cost decor pieces you can make. Tissue-paper pomanders and cardstock peonies photograph as lush florals from even a few feet away.

Materials needed:

  • Tissue paper in your palette (8-10 packs) — Dollar Tree, $1 per pack
  • Cardstock (for structured flowers) — Michaels, $5 for a 50-sheet pad
  • Floral wire and tape — $4
  • Fishing line or clear command hooks — $3
  • A foam board, pegboard, or tension rod as the backing — $5
  • Scissors and a hot glue gun

Step-by-step:

  1. For tissue pomanders: stack 8 sheets of tissue paper, accordion-fold in 1.5-inch intervals, round the ends with scissors, and fan out each layer into a puff.
  2. For cardstock peonies: cut 5-7 petals in graduating sizes, curl the edges with a pencil, and hot-glue them layered around a folded center.
  3. Build a mix of large and small flowers to fill the backdrop densely.
  4. Attach flowers to your backing board with hot glue, or hang them at staggered heights from a tension rod using fishing line.
  5. Transport flat between layers of tissue paper to avoid crushing.

4. Wine Bottle Candle Holders

DIY cost: Free / upcycled  ·  Professional equivalent: $15+ each  ·  Savings: ~$15+ per bottle

Save wine bottles from friends and family for a few months (or ask a local restaurant to set empties aside) and you have an unlimited supply of elegant taper candle holders. A table runner lined with glowing bottles at different heights is moody, romantic, and entirely free.

Materials needed:

  • Empty wine bottles (collected free) — 5-8 per table
  • Taper candles — Dollar Tree, 6 for $1
  • Goo Gone or rubbing alcohol for label removal — $3
  • Optional: metallic spray paint or frosted glass paint — $6

Step-by-step:

  1. Soak bottles in hot soapy water for 20 minutes, then peel off labels and scrub off residue with Goo Gone.
  2. Optional: spray-paint bottles in a uniform metallic (gold, copper) or leave them natural green/clear for a vineyard look.
  3. Let paint cure for 24 hours in a dust-free spot.
  4. Insert a taper candle into each bottle neck; if it's loose, melt the candle base slightly and press in for a snug fit.
  5. Arrange in clusters of varying heights down the center of the table on a runner of greenery or burlap.

Safety note: Never leave burning candles unattended, and keep bottles away from flammable fabrics. For indoor venues with strict fire codes, swap tapers for battery LED tapers.

5. Fresh Eucalyptus Greenery Garlands

DIY cost: $20 for 10ft  ·  Professional equivalent: $80+ for 10ft  ·  Savings: ~$60 per 10ft

Florists charge $8-12 per foot for fresh greenery garlands. Buying bulk eucalyptus from a wholesale flower site or Costco and wiring it yourself cuts that to about $2 per foot — and eucalyptus is the most forgiving greenery to work with because it lasts out of water for a full day.

Materials needed:

  • Bulk silver dollar eucalyptus (5 bunches) — FiftyFlowers or Costco, ~$40 total (yields 20+ ft)
  • Floral wire (22-gauge) — $4
  • Floral tape — $3
  • Pruning shears — $8 (or borrow)

Step-by-step:

  1. Order eucalyptus to arrive 2 days before the wedding; store stems in buckets of water in a cool spot.
  2. Cut stems into 6-8 inch pieces the morning of setup.
  3. Take 3-4 pieces, stagger them, and bind the stems with floral wire to start the garland base.
  4. Layer the next cluster so its stems overlap and hide the previous wire joint; continue adding and wiring every few inches.
  5. Mist the finished garland with water and keep it in a cool, dark place until placement.
  6. Drape along the head table, weave through candle clusters, or frame the ceremony arch.

6. Photo Clothesline Display

DIY cost: $5  ·  Professional equivalent: $40+  ·  Savings: ~$35

A timeline of the couple's photos strung on twine with mini clothespins is a sweet, personal decor piece for the gift table or cocktail hour — and it costs less than a fancy greeting card.

Materials needed:

  • Jute twine — $4 for a roll
  • Mini wooden clothespins — Dollar Tree, $1 for a pack of 20
  • Printed photos (4x6, 20-30 prints) — Walmart or Shutterfly, ~$0.09 each
  • Command hooks or push pins (already on hand)

Step-by-step:

  1. Curate 20-30 photos spanning your relationship and print them in a uniform size.
  2. Tie twine between two command hooks, leaving a gentle droop for a relaxed look.
  3. Clip photos along the line at even intervals, alternating vertical and horizontal orientation.
  4. For longer displays, run 2-3 parallel lines at different heights.
  5. String a few fairy lights along the same line for evening glow.

7. Painted Wood Slice Table Numbers

DIY cost: $2 each  ·  Professional equivalent: $8+ each  ·  Savings: ~$6+ per number

Rustic wood slices with hand-painted numbers are a 10-minute project that saves $6+ per table. For a 20-table wedding, that's $120 saved on table numbers alone.

Materials needed:

  • Wood slices (3-4 inch diameter) — Amazon or craft store, ~$1 each in bulk
  • White or gold paint pen — $5 (shared across all numbers)
  • Optional: clear sealant spray — $6

Step-by-step:

  1. Wipe each wood slice clean and let it dry.
  2. Lightly sketch the number in pencil first if you want a guide.
  3. Trace and fill in with the paint pen, letting the grain show through for a handcrafted look.
  4. Optional: spray with a clear sealant so the paint doesn't smudge in transit.
  5. Display each on a small ring of greenery or a doily at the table's center.

8. DIY Flower Wall Using Artificial Flowers

DIY cost: $40  ·  Professional equivalent: $300+  ·  Savings: ~$260

A full flower wall behind the sweetheart table or photo booth is the most photographed backdrop at most weddings — and renting one runs $300-600. A DIY version using artificial flowers from Amazon looks nearly identical in photos and lasts forever as a keepsake.

Materials needed:

  • Artificial flowers (150-200 stems) — Amazon, ~$30 for a bulk pack
  • Foam board or pegboard backing — $8
  • Hot glue gun and 30+ glue sticks — $6
  • Wire cutters — $5 (or borrow)
  • Command strips for hanging — $3

Step-by-step:

  1. Cut flower stems to 2-3 inches, leaving enough to anchor into the foam.
  2. Start at one corner and hot-glue the largest blooms first, spacing them evenly.
  3. Fill gaps between large flowers with medium and small blooms, angling some outward for dimension.
  4. Add greenery sprigs last to soften edges and hide any visible foam.
  5. Attach Command strips to the back and mount on the wall the morning of the wedding (two people make this much easier).

9. Lantern Aisle Runners with LED Candles

DIY cost: $8 per lantern  ·  Professional equivalent: $25+ per lantern  ·  Savings: ~$17+ per lantern

Lining the ceremony aisle with lanterns gives every guest a framed view of the vows and photographs beautifully at golden hour. Buying plain metal lanterns on sale and outfitting them with LED candles costs a third of renting.

Materials needed:

  • Metal or wood lanterns (10-12 for a full aisle) — Michaels with a 40% coupon or Amazon, ~$8 each
  • LED pillar candles (one per lantern) — Dollar Tree, $1 each
  • Optional: rose petals or greenery to scatter at the base — $4

Step-by-step:

  1. Buy lanterns in two heights for visual rhythm down the aisle.
  2. Drop an LED pillar candle into each lantern and switch on before guests arrive.
  3. Space lanterns evenly along both sides of the aisle, weighting the base with a hidden stone if outdoors.
  4. Scatter petals or greenery at the base of every other lantern for a fuller look.
  5. Recruit a helper to switch all candles off at the end of the night to save batteries.

10. Champagne Tower Setup

DIY cost: $0 if using provided glasses  ·  Professional equivalent: $50+ rental  ·  Savings: ~$50

A champagne tower is pure theater and almost always provided for free if your venue or caterer supplies coupes. The "DIY" here is really about coordination — you arrange the glasses and source the bubbly yourself rather than paying a rental company to stage it.

Materials needed:

  • Coupe or flute glasses (borrowed from venue/caterer — usually free with bar package)
  • 2-3 bottles of prosecco or champagne — Costco Kirkland prosecco, ~$7/bottle
  • A sturdy, level table with a non-slip cloth

Step-by-step:

  1. Confirm with your caterer that coupes are included in your bar package (most include them at no charge).
  2. Build the tower on a level table the day of: a square base of 6x6 glasses (36), then 5x5 (25), then 4x4 (16), then 3x3 (9), then 2x2 (4), topped with a single glass — 91 glasses total.
  3. Check each layer is level before adding the next.
  4. Pour slowly into the top glass and let it cascade down — practice once with water if you're nervous.
  5. Time the pour for the toast, then hand out the filled coupes.

11. Custom Neon Sign Alternative (LED Rope Light)

DIY cost: $20  ·  Professional equivalent: $150+  ·  Savings: ~$130

Custom neon signs reading "Better Together" or your new last name are a huge trend — and a huge expense at $150-400. An LED neon rope light bent into the same word gives the same glow for $20, and you can shape it into anything.

Materials needed:

  • Flexible LED neon rope light (16ft, warm white or color) — Amazon, ~$18
  • Clear command hooks or zip ties — $3
  • A plywood or foam-board backing (optional) — $4
  • Printed template of your word in a continuous-line font

Step-by-step:

  1. Print your word in a large, continuous-line font (single stroke) on several taped-together pages.
  2. Tape the template to your backing board or directly to the wall.
  3. Place clear command hooks along the letter outlines at 3-inch intervals.
  4. Bend the LED rope light to follow the template, threading it through the hooks.
  5. Plug in and dim the room lights to check the glow, adjusting hooks as needed.

12. Seating Chart on a Mirror or Window Frame

DIY cost: $10 thrift  ·  Professional equivalent: $60+  ·  Savings: ~$50

A vintage mirror or salvaged window frame with the seating chart written in elegant calligraphy is a statement piece that doubles as decor. Thrift stores are full of large mirrors and old window frames for under $10.

Materials needed:

  • Large vintage mirror or multi-pane window frame — thrift store or Facebook Marketplace, $5-10
  • Chalk marker or liquid chalk pen — $5
  • Windex and microfiber cloth — on hand
  • Easel (borrow or buy used) — $10

Step-by-step:

  1. Clean the mirror thoroughly so the marker writes smoothly.
  2. Lightly sketch your layout in sections: a header, then table numbers with guest names beneath each.
  3. Write the seating chart with a chalk marker (it wipes off glass easily with a damp cloth, so mistakes are fixable).
  4. For window frames, tape printed name cards into each pane instead.
  5. Display on an easel near the reception entrance with a small "Find Your Seat" sign.

13. DIY Bouquet Using Grocery Store Flowers

DIY cost: $15-30  ·  Professional equivalent: $150-400  ·  Savings: ~$135-370

The bridal bouquet is the single most photographed floral arrangement of the day — and florists charge $150-400 for it. A grocery store bouquet from Trader Joe's or Costco, built the morning of, can look just as lush for $15-30 if you follow a simple structure.

Materials needed:

  • 3-4 grocery store bouquets (mix of roses, carnations, eucalyptus) — Trader Joe's, ~$8 each
  • 1 statement flower bundle (peony or ranunculus if in season) — $10
  • Floral tape and ribbon — $5
  • Rubber bands and a straight pin — on hand

Step-by-step:

  1. Buy flowers 1-2 days before, re-cut stems, and store in water in the fridge (not with fruit, which wilts them).
  2. The morning of, strip all leaves below the bloom line and lay out flowers sorted by type.
  3. Build a base of 3 statement flowers in a triangle, then add roses around them in a spiral, turning the bouquet as you go.
  4. Tuck carnations and greenery (eucalyptus) around the outer edge to frame.
  5. Tighten with a rubber band at the grip point, trim stems to a uniform length, and wrap the handle with ribbon secured by a straight pin.
  6. Keep in a vase of water until 30 minutes before the ceremony, then pat dry.

Pro tip: Practice once with cheap flowers a week before so you're not learning on wedding morning.

14. Tulle and Fairy Light Ceremony Arch

DIY cost: $30  ·  Professional equivalent: $200+  ·  Savings: ~$170

Renting a decorated ceremony arch runs $200-500. A simple PVC or wooden arch frame draped in tulle and woven with fairy lights costs $30 to build and looks ethereal in photos — especially for evening or indoor ceremonies.

Materials needed:

  • PVC pipe arch kit or wooden branches lashed together — $15
  • 2-3 bolts of tulle in your color — $1/yard at Dollar Tree, ~$10 total
  • 2-3 strings of fairy lights (battery or plug-in) — $12 for a multi-pack
  • Zip ties and floral wire — $3
  • Optional: 1 eucalyptus bunch for greenery accents — $5

Step-by-step:

  1. Assemble the arch frame and anchor the base in weighted buckets or sandbags hidden by fabric.
  2. Drape tulle in loose swags across the top, securing with zip ties at the corners.
  3. Weave fairy lights through the tulle, hiding battery packs behind the frame.
  4. Add eucalyptus sprigs at the top corners and base for organic texture.
  5. Test the lights at the venue's lighting level and adjust drape fullness before the ceremony.

15. Personalized Favor Bags with Stamps

DIY cost: $1 each  ·  Professional equivalent: $4+ each  ·  Savings: ~$3+ per guest

Custom-stamped favor bags filled with a small treat (a cookie, a packet of seeds, or a mini candle) feel intentional and personal — and at $1 each, they cost a quarter of the personalized favors from Etsy.

Materials needed:

  • Kraft paper bags (one per guest) — Amazon, ~$0.15 each
  • Custom rubber stamp with your monogram or "Thank You" — Etsy, ~$15 (one-time cost)
  • Stamp pad in your wedding color — $5
  • Favor filler (bulk cookies, seed packets, or mini candles) — ~$0.50-0.80 each
  • Ribbon or twine to tie — $3

Step-by-step:

  1. Order your custom stamp 6-8 weeks ahead (Etsy turnaround is 2-3 weeks).
  2. Stamp each bag in a consistent spot, pressing firmly and lifting straight up to avoid smudging.
  3. Let ink dry for 10 minutes before stacking.
  4. Fill each bag with your favor filler the week of the wedding.
  5. Fold the top over, punch two holes, and thread ribbon through in a bow.
  6. Arrange in a basket or tray at the exit with a small "Take One" sign.

Where to Buy Materials Cheaply

Where you source materials matters as much as what you make. The same project can cost 3x more at a premium craft store than at a dollar store. Here's where to stock up:

  • Dollar Tree — The MVP of budget wedding supplies. Mason jars, fairy lights, taper candles, mini clothespins, tulle by the yard, and glass vases are all $1.25 each. Buy online by the case for bulk quantities your local store may not stock.
  • Michaels — Best for craft basics (cardstock, paint pens, floral wire, wood slices). Always stack a 40% off any single item coupon (sign up for the app) on top of sale prices. Watch for the frequent "buy one get two free" floral sales.
  • Amazon — Cheapest for bulk artificial flowers, LED neon rope lights, kraft favor bags, and fairy light multi-packs. Read reviews carefully for color accuracy and check delivery dates against your timeline.
  • Thrift stores (Goodwill, Salvation Army, local shops) — Source for vintage mirrors, window frames, glassware, lanterns, and easels. Visit several locations over a few weeks since inventory rotates constantly. Bring a friend and a measuring tape.
  • Facebook Marketplace & Craigslist — The single best source for "wedding decorations" lots from couples who just got married. You can often buy an entire decor package (arch, lanterns, signage, vases) for $100-300, then resell it after your wedding for nearly the same amount. Also great for free pallets and used easels.
  • Costco & Sam's Club — Best for bulk fresh flowers, eucalyptus, and prosecco for the champagne tower. Costco's wedding flower packages run $300-700 for 150 guests versus $2,000-5,000 from a florist.
  • FiftyFlowers & Bloomex (wholesale online) — Direct-to-consumer bulk flowers and greenery shipped overnight. Ideal for the DIY bouquet and eucalyptus garlands.
  • Trader Joe's — Cheap, high-quality grocery store bouquets for DIY bouquets and bud vases. Shop the morning of for the freshest picks, and ask the florist on duty for bulk pricing.

One more tip: always check the clearance section at every store. Craft stores clearance out seasonal colors (pastels after Easter, metallics after Christmas) that work perfectly for weddings at 70-90% off.

What NOT to DIY

Not every wedding element is worth doing yourself. Some projects are genuinely risky, logistically impossible, or simply not worth the stress on a day when you should be enjoying yourself. Skip DIY for these:

  • Fresh floral installations that need to survive all day — Large fresh flower arches, chuppahs, and ceiling installations wilt without professional hydration and structural engineering. A drooping arch at the altar is the kind of thing you can't fix mid-ceremony. Rent or hire a florist for these.
  • Your own hair and makeup — The pressure of doing your own glam on a timeline, in photos that last forever, is not worth the $150-300 savings. Book a pro or a talented friend, but don't go solo.
  • Food for the full reception — Catering for 150 people requires commercial kitchen capacity, health permits, and hot holding equipment. DIY appetizers or a dessert bar are fine; DIY dinner is a food-safety and logistics nightmare. (See our catering savings guide instead.)
  • Photography and videography — There is no DIY version of your wedding photos. A friend with a nice camera is not a wedding photographer. This is the one category where you should never skimp; the photos are the only thing that lasts beyond the day.
  • The wedding cake (for large guest counts) — A small cutting cake is fine to DIY, but a structurally sound multi-tier cake that feeds 150 is genuinely hard. The "display cake + sheet cake" trick from our catering guide is the smart compromise.
  • Anything involving open flame near flammable decor — Real candles in venues without fire clearance, fireworks, or sparklers indoors are insurance and safety risks. Use LED alternatives; they're indistinguishable in photos.
  • Audio / DJ equipment — A Bluetooth speaker works for a 30-person backyard wedding. For 100+ guests, you need a real PA, a microphone that doesn't feedback, and someone managing the playlist and cues. Hire a DJ or at least a day-of coordinator with AV experience.
  • Complex floral foam structures — Centerpieces in vases are forgiving. Large floral foam installations (crescents, overhead florals, table-length runners with mechanics) need professional construction or they collapse. Stick to vase-based DIY florals.

The test is simple: if failure would be visible, unfixable on the day, or unsafe, hire a professional. For everything else, the 15 projects above are fair game.
