What the Average Wedding Videographer Cost Looks Like in 2026

Most of the spread comes down to three variables: how many shooters, how long they stay, and how much finished runtime you get back. Here is the market in four tiers, which is how our calculator is built.

Against the average US wedding of about $34,200 (The Knot 2026 Real Weddings Study), that typical $2,500 is roughly 7% of the total, and a $4,500 cinematic package is about 13%. For a second reference point, photography averages near $2,900, so the two visual vendors usually cost close to the same money. The $1,500 entry point is real, but read the fine print. It is almost always six hours, one shooter, and a reel only. If the ceremony matters to you (it usually does), the honest floor is closer to $2,500.

Where the Money Goes Inside a $2,800 Quote

Studios rarely price by the hour on your timeline. They price the labor it takes to shoot and finish the film. Here is a typical $2,800 mid-tier job, broken into what the studio actually spends.

Line Amount What It Covers
Lead shooter, 8 hours$850Day rate, not hourly. Most freelancers quote $600–$1,200 for a Saturday.
Second shooter$400The other angles: reaction shots, guest candids, a backup on the vows.
Edit, audio mix, color$1,05030–35 editor hours at a blended $30–$35 rate, whether in-house or contracted out.
Music license$60A licensed track from a catalog like Musicbed or Artlist. Pop songs cannot be cleared.
Gear wear, insurance$120Two bodies, three lenses, wireless lavs, recorder, liability policy.
Travel and parking$80Local mileage, garage fees, the second car if there are two shooters.
Admin and processing$90Card fees, contracts, client communication, gallery hosting.
Studio margin$150About 5% on this job. Thin margins are why deposits are non-refundable.

The edit line is the one couples underrate. A six-minute reel comes from roughly 90 minutes of ceremony, 40 minutes of toasts, and several hundred gigabytes of reception footage. Selecting clips, syncing three audio sources, cutting to music, color grading, and exporting takes 30 to 45 hours at most studios. That is a 5-to-1 ratio against shoot hours, and it is the single best predictor of whether a quote comes back at $1,700 or $4,000.

A cheap quote usually means one of three things: fewer editor hours (a stiff, ungraded cut), fewer shooters (one angle all night), or a rushed turnover. Ask which one they trimmed. A studio that can answer plainly is a studio worth hiring.

Four Ways Videographers Price a Wedding

  • Hourly. $200–$350 per hour with a four-hour minimum. Common for elopements and courthouse coverage, rare for full weddings.
  • Package. A fixed price for a bundle of hours, shooters, and deliverables. The most common structure, and the hardest to compare, because the bundle hides what an extra hour would cost.
  • Coverage fee plus overtime. A base for eight hours, then $150–$250 per hour after that. Our calculator uses $150 per hour past the eight-hour mark, which is the low end of market.
  • À la carte. A base coverage charge, then each deliverable priced separately: reel $800, ceremony edit $400, vertical social cut $150, raw footage $300. Best for control, most work to compare.

If you want to negotiate without friction, ask for the package plus a written overtime rate. That combination gives you a predictable total and a fair price if the night runs long, which it usually does.

Why the Average Wedding Videographer Cost Climbs With the Edit

Two quotes for eight hours of coverage can differ by $3,000 without either studio lying. The difference lives in the finished product.

A four-minute reel needs one music pass, a rough cut, and a single revision round. A 40-minute documentary needs the ceremony cut clean, every toast trimmed, vows mixed against room noise, a two-camera sync, and two revision rounds. Add a same-day edit and the studio has to staff a second editor on site, working on a laptop in a back room during dinner. That is why the same-day cut adds $1,500–$2,500 rather than a few hundred.

Audio is where inexpensive video falls apart, and it is invisible on a quote. A proper setup runs a lav on the officiant, a lav on each partner, and a feed from the DJ's board, recorded redundantly. Outdoor ceremonies add wind protection. If a quote does not mention audio at all, assume a single on-camera mic and vows you can barely hear over the breeze.

Before you commit, decide which runtime you will actually rewatch. Nearly everyone watches a five-minute reel more than once. Very few watch the 90-minute version twice.

Cost per Finished Minute: The Check Nobody Runs

Divide the quote by the minutes you get back and the tiers stop looking like a ladder. They start looking like a curve.

Deliverable Price Runtime Per Minute
Highlight reel$1,8004 min$450
Ceremony + reel$2,80036 min$78
Documentary film$4,20020 min$210
Long-form cut$5,00060 min$83

The lesson is not "buy the most minutes." It is that minutes are almost free once the ceremony is already being cut, so a quote that charges $1,400 extra for a full ceremony edit is charging for labor, not for length. And a reel priced at $450 per minute is not a rip-off either. That number reflects the same 30 hours of work spread across four minutes.

Where the check earns its keep is spotting an outlier. If a studio wants $3,600 for an eight-minute reel with no ceremony and one shooter, ask what the extra $1,500 buys. Sometimes there is an answer. Sometimes there is a sales call.

Add-Ons Priced Separately, and Which Ones Earn Their Line

These are the lines that show up after you have agreed on a package price. Ask for all of them in writing before you sign.

Add-On Typical Price Verdict
Raw footage$300Buy it. Get it on a drive, not a download link that expires in 90 days.
Drone coverage$400Only if the venue allows it and the ceremony is outdoors.
Same-day edit$1,500–$2,500Skip unless you are screening it that night.
Vertical social cut$150–$400Cheap enough to take if you post a lot. Otherwise skip.
Rush delivery$250–$600Only worth it if you need the film for a specific date.
Live stream$500–$1,500Priced by bandwidth and a second operator. Worth it for out-of-town family.
Travel beyond the metro$0.65–$1.00 per mile, or $200–$500 flatNegotiable. A studio already driving that way may waive it.

Drone footage deserves a caveat. The pilot needs a Part 107 certificate, and plenty of venues, state parks, and national park sites prohibit launches outright. Confirm with the venue before you pay for coverage you cannot legally shoot.

One more line item that never appears on a quote: the deposit. Expect 25–50% at signing and the balance due two weeks before the wedding, not after. Ask whether a card payment carries a 3% surcharge. On a $3,000 job that is $90 of pure friction you can avoid with a check.

Same Package, Different Zip Code

Geography moves video pricing more than most couples expect, mostly because shooter day rates track local labor costs.

  • New York, Los Angeles, San Francisco, Boston: $4,500–$8,000 for a full-day, two-shooter package.
  • Chicago, Seattle, Washington DC, Miami: $3,200–$5,500.
  • Austin, Dallas, Atlanta, Denver: $2,400–$4,200.
  • Columbus, Kansas City, Tulsa, and most secondary markets: $1,500–$2,800, often with the same two-person crew.

Driving 90 minutes outside a major metro can cut 25–35% off the same package. A destination wedding adds airfare plus two nights of lodging for each shooter, so a $3,000 film can become $4,200 before the ceremony starts.

How to Compare Two Quotes Line by Line

Put both quotes into one table with these eight fields. Every disagreement between them will explain itself.

  • Coverage clock. Does the eight hours start at the getting-ready suite or at the ceremony? That is two hours of difference.
  • Shooter count. One person cannot cover a 120-guest reception and the toasts happening at the same time.
  • Finished runtime. Minutes delivered, not hours filmed.
  • Revision rounds. Two is standard. Unlimited is a promise nobody keeps.
  • Music license. Confirm it covers public posting, or your film may get muted by a rights claim on social.
  • Raw footage. Included, extra, or unavailable.
  • Delivery date. Six to ten weeks is normal. Twelve to sixteen happens in October.
  • Overtime rate. In writing, per hour, and what triggers it.

Then run the total against what is left in your budget after venue and catering. The Wedding Budget Calculator shows that remainder directly, and the Vendor Comparison Tool will tell you whether a photo-plus-video bundle is genuinely cheaper than booking both separately. Bundles usually save 10–20%, but only when the studio is not quietly downgrading one of the two services to make the number look good.

Where the Real Savings Are

The cuts that work are structural, not negotiated. Six moves, biggest first:

  • Drop the same-day edit. Saves $1,500–$2,500 and costs you nothing you would rewatch.
  • Go to one shooter and six hours. Saves $700–$1,100. Keep the ceremony, cut the getting-ready coverage.
  • Book a Friday or Sunday. Studios discount 10–20% because demand drops. On a $3,000 package that is $300–$600 for a date change.
  • Hire an associate shooter. Many studios sell a package where a lead shooter's studio sends a trained associate for $1,200–$1,800, with the lead doing the edit. Same quality control, lower day rate.
  • Sign in December. Most studios raise rates 5–8% each January. A contract signed in the last weeks of the year locks the current price for a wedding a year out.
  • Trim the audio package, not the ceremony edit. A third wireless mic setup runs $100–$250. The ceremony edit is the piece you will actually watch, so protect it and cut elsewhere.

If you want one number to put in the spreadsheet, use $2,800. That buys eight hours, one shooter, the full ceremony and toasts, and a five-minute reel in most US markets outside the biggest metros. Treat $4,500 as your ceiling, and only cross it if you have seen a sample film from that studio that you would genuinely watch twice. Everything above that is buying art direction and runtime. Neither is a mistake. Both are optional.
