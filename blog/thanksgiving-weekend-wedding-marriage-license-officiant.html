Your wedding is Saturday, November 28, 2026. The county clerk closes Thursday for Thanksgiving and, at most offices, stays closed Friday. That leaves one working day to collect a marriage license: Wednesday, November 25.

The license costs $60 in Cook County. The officiant who signs it charges $650 that Saturday instead of $450 two weeks earlier. Add a separate rehearsal, a second drive to the venue, and a tip, and the legal end of a wedding runs $1,115 on Thanksgiving weekend against $585 on a quiet Saturday.

Everything else on your vendor list can be rescheduled. A license cannot be issued by a closed office.

What a Marriage License Costs, and Why the Fee Is the Small Part

Fees run $35 to $115 by county, and most sit between $60 and $90. Cook County is $60. Manhattan is $35. A rural county in the South is often $40 with a cash-only drawer and a sign taped to the counter.

Three rules apply almost everywhere. Both of you appear in person with government photo ID. The license is valid only in the state that issued it, so a New Jersey license does nothing for an Illinois ceremony. And it expires, usually in 60 days.

No state requires a blood test anymore, so that advice from your grandmother is obsolete. Counties do ask for a certified copy of a divorce decree if either of you married before.

Then the copies. The license you sign belongs to the officiant, who returns it to the clerk. What you keep is a certified copy, $5 to $15 each, and you want two or three for the name change, the driver's license, and the insurance form that will absolutely ask. Order them when you apply.

The buried cost is the trip. Marry in Illinois while living in Texas and somebody flies to Chicago to stand at a counter for twenty minutes. That flight is $280 to $340 in late November, which is why couples book it for the morning of November 25 rather than trusting a relative. Most clerks require both parties present.

This is the table worth printing, assuming a November 28 ceremony and a county clerk on the standard federal holiday schedule.

Date Office What has to happen

Mon, Nov 23 Open Last application day for a three-day wait

Two details on that Wednesday. Many county offices run a half day and shut at noon, and nearly all are appointment-only. It is also the busiest appointment day of the year at urban clerks, since every couple marrying over the long weekend stands in the same line. Book in October. Then ask the clerk to confirm your officiant's name and credential, because a misspelled last name means a second trip, and this week has no second trip.

Waiting Periods, Priced Against the Calendar

A waiting period is a delay between applying and being allowed to marry. It is why some couples are already late today.

  • Same day: California, Colorado, Texas, and most of the South and West. Apply Wednesday, marry Saturday.
  • One day: Illinois and New York, where the license takes effect the day after issuance. A Wednesday pickup covers Saturday.
  • Three days: Pennsylvania and Florida, which pushes the deadline to Monday, November 23. Florida waives the wait in some cases, so ask.
  • Six days: Wisconsin, which puts the application date at today. If you marry in Wisconsin on November 28 and have not applied, make that call first.

Waiting periods interact with the holiday in ways that are easy to miss. A three-day wait counts business days in some counties and calendar days in others. Illinois runs the clock from issuance, so a Wednesday license is effective Thursday, which is fine.

Two states let you skip the officiant entirely. Colorado allows self-solemnization, where the couple signs as their own officiants with no witness. Pennsylvania issues self-uniting licenses on request, a Quaker tradition any couple can use. Both cost the standard fee and remove one vendor, one rehearsal, and one $650 invoice from your week.

What a Thanksgiving Weekend Officiant Charges

Independent celebrants quote the Saturday after Thanksgiving the way they quote a June Saturday. Same officiant, same venue, same 88 guests, two dates.

Line Sat, Nov 14 Sat, Nov 28 Difference
License, county fee $60 $60 $0
Officiant fee $450 $650 +$200
Rehearsal, second trip included $200 +$200
Mileage, 150 mi at $0.70 $0 $105 +$105
Gratuity $75 $100 +$25
Total $585 $1,115 +$530

The $200 on the fee is supply and demand, not a surcharge with a name. A celebrant outside Chicago has four sellable Saturdays in November, and November 28 sits inside a four-day window when every venue within 30 miles is booked. She turns down two other couples to hold your date.

The rehearsal line is legitimate. On a normal weekend the rehearsal happens Friday at the venue and folds into the fee, because she is driving out anyway. On Thanksgiving weekend Friday belongs to your welcome dinner and the venue belongs to somebody else's event, so the rehearsal moves to Wednesday. That is a separate 75-mile round trip on a weeknight, and it gets billed.

Mileage at $0.70 for 150 miles is roughly what a car costs to run in 2026. Most independents bill a flat $75 to $150 travel fee instead, which is the same money described two ways. Ask which one you are getting.

The tip is smaller than people expect. Our wedding tipping guide puts a civil officiant at $50 to $100, with no percentage convention because there is no service staff. For clergy the gift is a donation to the house of worship, $100 to $500, and that one is not optional in the way a tip is.

Four Officiant Options, Priced for This Date

Most couples pick a person, then discover the paperwork problem. On a holiday weekend, pick the option that survives a closed clerk's office first.

Option All-in What you get Right call when
Ordained friend $60 to $100 Free or $40 ordination, plus the fee Under 100 guests, and the friend is the point
Religious officiant $160 to $560 Ceremony, counseling, rehearsal, donation You belong to the congregation already
Professional celebrant $855 to $1,055 Custom vows, two calls, rehearsal, travel Nobody in the family can hold a room of 88
Courthouse $60 to $200 Ten minutes with a judge, weekdays only Date legal, party separate

The friend route is cheapest by a wide margin and carries one real risk. Online ordination is free through Universal Life Church and about $40 through American Marriage Ministries, and in most states that credential is all your friend needs. Two exceptions. A few states have refused to recognize online ordinations, and some cities require the officiant to register locally first. New York City requires that in writing, weeks ahead.

The religious route is cheap only if you are already a member. Many parishes want six months' notice and a preparation course, which rules out a date eight days away. If a parent is arranging this in November, the answer is probably no, and December 1 is a bad day to find out.

The courthouse deserves more respect than it gets. A judge, a Tuesday morning, $60 to $200, and a lunch afterward costs a tenth of the celebrant line. It cannot happen on a weekend, so the legal ceremony lands Wednesday, the same day you collect the license.

The Rehearsal Moves to Wednesday, and Somebody Pays for It

On an ordinary weekend the rehearsal is Friday at 5:30, dinner at 7:00. Thanksgiving takes both slots. Thursday is a 20-pound bird and eleven relatives. Friday is your welcome dinner, which on this weekend runs $5,992 for 72 seats at a restaurant charging holiday prix fixe.

So the rehearsal lands Wednesday, November 25, at the same hour you are supposed to be at the clerk. Book the license for 9:00 a.m. and the rehearsal for 4:00 p.m., and the day works.

What changes is who arrives early. A Friday rehearsal lets the party fly in Friday morning. A Wednesday rehearsal puts the party and both sets of parents on the ground Tuesday night, in a hotel, on the second-most expensive travel day of the year. Our transportation breakdown counts 74 of 88 guests in a hotel. Fifteen of them now need a fourth night.

A room runs $287 once 14.5% tax and a $25 amenity fee land on it, per our guest cost breakdown. Twelve rooms for the party and immediate family, one extra Tuesday: $3,446. Your guests pay it, not you, which is why it never appears in your spreadsheet and still shapes how the week feels to the people beside you.

One decision cuts most of it. Keep the rehearsal to 35 minutes at 4:00 Wednesday and skip the dinner, because Friday already has one. Or hold it Saturday at 10:00 in the empty venue, which costs nothing and buys back the hotel night for twelve people.

Where $1,115 Lands in a $34,200 Budget

The national average wedding costs $34,200 in 2026, per The Knot's Real Weddings Study. The $1,115 legal line is 3.3% of that. The license fee inside it is 0.2%. Slightly more than a third of one percent decides whether the wedding is legal.

Templates handle this badly. A typical one lists officiant at $300 to $500 and license at $60, accurate for a Saturday in April and short by $540 here. Our 150-guest budget breakdown groups them at $360 to $560.

Two lines are fixed and two are not. The $60 fee is the same in every Illinois county and does not move for anyone. The $105 mileage is arithmetic. The $650 fee and the $200 rehearsal are negotiation, and the honest way to negotiate a holiday date is one question: what does this cost on November 14? An officiant quoting $650 for both dates is telling you $650 is her normal rate, which is useful. One quoting $450 and $650 has shown you the premium.
